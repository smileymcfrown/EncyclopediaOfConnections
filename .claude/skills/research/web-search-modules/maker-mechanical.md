# Maker & Mechanical Design Module

> Added for the Encyclopedia of Connections project (not part of the upstream skill).

**When to use**: researching mechanical connections, joints, fasteners, snap fits, hinges,
bearings, linkages, compliant mechanisms, or 3D-printing / CNC / robotics design techniques.

## Sources (roughly in priority order)
- **Existing encyclopedias, wikis and digital libraries of mechanisms**: e.g. the Digital
  Mechanism and Gear Library (DMG-Lib), Cornell KMODDL (Reuleaux models), *507 Mechanical
  Movements*, Wikipedia category trees, and GitHub repos, datasets and CAD/OpenSCAD libraries of
  joints and mechanisms. Check these first, because they often already list and illustrate the type.
- **Manufacturer & service design guides**: plastics makers' snap-fit and design manuals
  (e.g. Covestro/Bayer, BASF, DuPont), 3D-printing and machining services (Prusa knowledge
  base, Formlabs, Protolabs, Xometry, Hubs), fastener/insert makers (e.g. heat-set insert
  datasheets). These are authoritative and usually illustrated.
- **Illustrated references**: *507 Mechanical Movements*, Nguyen Duc Thang's mechanism
  animations, BYU Compliant Mechanisms Research, Wikipedia (and its images on Wikimedia Commons, which are openly licensed)
- **Supplier catalogues for vocabulary**: McMaster-Carr, Misumi, igus. Good for the proper
  names and variants of off-the-shelf parts.
- **Maker communities**: r/functionalprint, r/3Dprinting, r/3DPrintingDesign, r/robotics,
  r/AskEngineers, r/MechanicalEngineering, r/Fusion360, Hackaday, Printables and Thingiverse
  (search for parametric/test-print models), Instructables.
- **Video**: YouTube channels that explain design techniques with demonstrations (e.g.
  CNC Kitchen for strength testing of 3D-printed joints and inserts).

## Query strategies
- Search **both** the proper term and plain beginner descriptions (e.g. "cantilever snap fit"
  AND "3d print clip that clicks shut"). Beginner wording finds forum threads; proper terms
  find design guides.
- Add process words to find practical advice: "3d printed", "FDM", "print in place",
  "tolerance", "clearance", "PLA/PETG", "CNC", "laser cut".
- Look for **images, diagrams, animations or videos** first, since the audience learns visually.
  Note the license (Wikimedia Commons, CC-licensed models and so on) whenever you can see it.
- For rules of thumb (dimensions, clearances, strain limits) prefer design guides and
  testing videos over forum opinion, and always cite the source.
- Only report URLs you actually opened. Never construct or guess a URL.
