## Candidate findings

### C1. EVA bevelled seam and V-groove fold (opened sources)
- costumary.com/blog/eva-foam-clean-builds: "Beveling is cutting the edge of foam at an angle so pieces join at curves instead of meeting at hard corners. Valley cuts (also called channel cuts) are partial-depth cuts on the back of foam that let it bend without creasing." "Cut at a 45-degree angle"; "On 10mm foam, a bevel creates a natural glue channel"; valley cuts "halfway through the foam's thickness on the back side", "Space valley cuts 2-3cm apart"; "Don't cut too deep or the foam will fold instead of curve." Contact cement: "you get one chance"; hot glue "rigid, melts in heat"; "Heat-form the curve first, then glue"; respirator with organic vapour cartridges.
- foodandcosplay.org (2015) part 2, "Folded edges": draw fold line on the underside, "cut out a strip of the foam on either side at a 45 degree angle of the fold line", glue the gap, fold, hold until dry (= V-groove fold, one-piece, no seam on the face).
- kamuicosplay.com 2017 foamcos: contact cement thin layers both sides, let dry, press; "EVA foam holds fine with velcro or even magnets"; D-rings for strapping mentioned in comments.
- cosplayadvice.com: contact cement, superglue or hot glue; hot glue not advised if heat-forming after; Kwik Seal caulk to fill seam holes after heat shaping.
=> Candidate new entry: V-groove fold (mitre fold) cross-material (EVA, foam board, also card/ACM). Bevel seam = glued mitre/butt joint: better as notes in Glued joint design (270) + contact adhesive (224)? Decide at end.

### C2. Foam-board folds and rabbet joints (opened sources)
- foldnfly.com/lounge/foam-core-tools-techniques.php: Score and Fold ("cut through the first layer of paper and some of the foam, but not into the bottom layer of paper ... leaving one layer of paper attached, like a hinge"); Beveled Cut Folds (45 degree cuts so the fold closes into the cut, hides the stair-step); Overlap Fold (two parallel cuts one board-thickness apart, 4-5 mm, peel out the foam strip, the paper skin wraps the corner, hot glue); "Regular super glue has chemicals in it that can melt foam"; hot glue favourite; clear packing tape and fibre tape to reinforce seams; toothpicks "poked through several layers of foam board to make a strong connection. They can also be used to make joints that can freely pivot or swivel."
- boards.core77.com/t/how-do-you-join-foam-core-edges/29942: rabbet cutters "work better than mitered edges"; cube with three 45 degree mitres and a rabbet, hot glue; "2 slits almost all the way through to the second sheet, 1/8" apart and remove the foam" for 90 degree corners; 3/16" rabbet round a perimeter plus kerf-cut strip for curves; a poster suggests gasoline to lift paper (UNSAFE: do not repeat).
- flitetest.com FT Old Fogey scratch build: A-style vs B-style folds; hot glue spread into the crease "will create a rubber-like hinge"; BBQ skewers through the fuselage hold the wing on with rubber bands; comments: skewer holes tear out, reinforce with packing tape, card or popsicle-stick doublers.
- flitetest.com Fowl Flyer: 'A-style' fold sets side walls on top of the bottom; 'B-style' fold, side wall against the bottom, self-squaring.
- en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foamcore: material background.

### C3. RC control-surface hinges in foam (opened sources)
- flitetest.com/articles/RC_Plane_Hinge_Types: Pin hinges ("look much like door hinges", pegged with a toothpick through the arm, epoxy, larger gas planes); Iron-on hinges (strips of covering film, in pairs); Tape hinges ("usually made from a strip of clear packing tape", top strip with surface fully down, under strip with surface fully up; "limited to electric planes made of foam"; "tape tends to lose adhesion over time"). Comments: "foam cut partially through makes a great hinge"; CA hinges "small rectangles of fuzz covered plastic ... slotted into the foam or balsa then fixed in place with a few drops of CA glue".
- library.modelaviation.com FAQ 2010/02 (Q432 hinge tape): tape hinges mostly for non-fuelled models; softer tape for light models; 3M Blenderm "works well on micro/indoor aircraft under 8 ounces"; SR gapless hinge tape for 8-48 oz; bevel for top hinging; gap: "Too little space causes binding ... too much causes flapping or vibration"; 1/16" balsa spacers.
- modelairplanenews.com/articles/how-to-hinge-foamies: "the method you choose will depend on the type and thickness of the foam"; "first step when hinging any surface is to bevel the edges"; 45 degree hinge line jig; Robart Hinge Points drilled with a Dremel. (Rest paywalled.)
- modelairplanenews.com/articles/ca-hinging-help: fresh thin CA, align slots, check fit to halfway point, drill relief holes and pin each hinge (page summary: "drill 1/16-in. relief holes, and pin each hinge before gluing"). (Rest paywalled.)
- library.modelaviation.com/article/pap-e (foam-board plane, 2000): hinge = "two parallel 3/8-inch cuts through the foam board, approximately 1/8-inch apart. Cut at an angle, but don't go through the last layer of paper, because it forms the hinge."
=> Candidates: Tape hinge (new, cross-material); slot-in model hinge (CA hinge + hinge points; new, or notes to knuckle hinge 263); paper-skin hinge = variant of score-and-fold (paper group); cut-foam hinge = living hinge (49) tag.

### C4. Glues for foam (opened sources)
- UHU por technical data sheet (PDF at static.rapidonline.com/pdf/64-1845t_v1.pdf): "quick-drying contact adhesive, which was specially developed for the bonding of polystyrene foam"; base synthetic rubber; "Solvent: Aliphatic solvent mixture"; flashpoint approx. -5 C; "The composition of its solvents is such that the structure of the polystyrene foam is not attacked"; firm adhesion 5-20 minutes after application; finger test. => UHU por is NOT solvent-free (draft list was wrong): it is a solvent contact adhesive whose solvents spare polystyrene.
- gluegun.com/blogs/tips-tutorials/adhesives-for-polystyrene: EPS and XPS "sensitive to solvents and heat"; "Expanded polystyrene is also more vulnerable to solvent damage"; safe classes: foam-safe spray adhesives, water-based adhesives, low-temperature foam-safe hot melts, compatible polyurethane adhesives; don't clean with alcohol or acetone. (Supplier page, sells these.)
- studiomatrx.org model-making page: "PVA is foam-safe; solvent glue melts foam; superglue fogs acrylic"; "glue gun (bulky parts, too hot for thin foam)"; "glue and hold with clamps/pins"; butt, mitre 45 degree and tab-and-slot joints in foamboard.
- en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foamcore: superglue and some paints make the foam "melt away and dissolve" [citation needed]; water in glue can warp the paper; spray adhesives best; hot glue as substitute.
- foldnfly: "Regular super glue has chemicals in it that can melt foam, so always use foam-compatible adhesives."
=> Not a new type (selection guide by foam). Handle as notes additions to contact adhesive (224: polystyrene-safe solvent contact glues of the UHU por kind), hot-melt (141: low-temperature guns for foam board and EPS), PVA (223: XPS terrain, needs one porous side or long cure), PU glue (225, already lists insulation board). Possibly a guide page "Gluing foam" later.

### C6. V-groove fold across materials (opened sources)
- alucobond.com.sg routing-and-folding: "A V-shaped or rectangular groove is routed on the reverse side ... A thin layer of the core material should be left at the base of the groove ... The untouched outer cover sheet can now be bent manually, giving an exact and clean folding line".
- woodweb.com knowledge base "Miterfolds for clean corner joints": "We do mitre folds by 45ing the material face up with a table saw. Place the two edges close together, tape with heavy packaging tape on the face, flip, glue, fold." Tape too aggressive can pull veneer. Brittle materials: cut right through "and just slightly into the tape".
- finewoodworking.com plywood drawers and trays: V-groove bit, cut "just short of breaking through the opposite side" (Roy Underhill hand method), glue in the grooves, fold, blue tape on the back of the grooves to avoid breakage.
- foodandcosplay.org (EVA), foldnfly (foam board "Beveled Cut Folds"), core77 (foam board) as above.
=> New entry: V-groove fold (mitre fold): one-piece corner where a V is cut from the back leaving the face skin as the hinge, then glued shut. Covers EVA foam, foam board, plywood/MDF, ACM panels, laminate; kerf/valley cuts for curves = Kerf bending (244) cross-ref.

## 4. Notes additions to existing entries

1. **224 Contact adhesive.** (a) Polystyrene-safe solvent contact glue: UHU por's data sheet says it is a
   synthetic-rubber contact adhesive with an "Aliphatic solvent mixture", whose solvents leave
   polystyrene foam unharmed. It is highly flammable (flash point about -5 °C), and you join when it
   passes the touch-dry finger test, 5-20 minutes after spreading. So the rule is "no ketone or aromatic
   solvents (acetone, MEK, toluene) on polystyrene", not "no solvent at all". (b) EVA seams: bevel both
   edges at about 45° (Costumary); form curves by heat before gluing, because stretching while gluing
   pulls seams apart later (Costumary); hot glue as a backup is not advised if the piece will be
   heat-formed afterwards (cosplayadvice.com). (c) Straps on foam armour: glue webbing ends with contact
   cement (xposeddesigns.com: hook side glued "with contact cement or epoxy").
2. **138 Two-part epoxy.** Solvent-free, so safe on EPS and XPS foam, unlike polyester and vinylester
   resins, which contain styrene and melt them. A pool of fast-setting epoxy can still get hot enough
   to soften foam, so use slow epoxy (Swaylocks thread). Use slow epoxy, not 5-minute epoxy or CA, for
   hinge points (Airfield Models).
3. **141 Hot-melt adhesive.** On foam board, a bead inside a grooved fold, held at angle for a few
   seconds, gives "a very sturdy joint" (foldnfly). Spread into a score-and-fold crease, it makes "a
   rubber-like hinge" that stops the paper tearing (Flite Test Old Fogey). It softens when EVA is
   heat-formed later (cosplayadvice.com). Low-temperature or "foam-safe" hot melts for EPS and XPS
   (gluegun.com).
4. **223 PVA.** Foam-safe for foam board and XPS terrain (Studio Matrx). Forum claims seen only in
   search results (not opened) say PVA cures slowly or not at all between two non-porous foam faces ⚑.
5. **282 Elastic band springs.** Rubber-band wing hold-down on model planes: several bands over dowels or
   skewers, "One rubber band failure out of six will not crash your airplane"; replace after every
   session (fuel and UV); never use strip rubber (Model Aviation FAQ 2013/04).
6. **299 Housing and rebate joints.** Foam-board rabbet: remove the foam to one board-thickness but keep
   the outer paper, so the paper covers the exposed foam edge; dedicated foam-board rabbet cutters exist
   (Core77).
7. **270 Glued joint design.** The bevelled (mitred) seam in EVA foam is the foam-cosplay version of
   the angled glue joint; it hides the seam line and gives more glue area (Costumary; foodandcosplay).
8. **49 Living hinge.** Partly cut foam (EPO, Depron, foam board) as a crude living hinge on scratch-built
   model planes (Flite Test comment); it stiffens and tires, so tape or CA hinges are used for anything
   lasting.

## 6. Safety points and figures that disagree

- **UHU por "solvent-free" (draft) vs data sheet:** the data sheet says "Aliphatic solvent mixture", "Highly
  flammable", flash point about -5 °C. It is foam-safe because of which solvent it uses, not because it
  has none.
- **Polyester resin on XPS:** one Swaylocks poster says "with xps foam you can use both [epoxy and
  polyester] as well". Others in the same thread say EPS and XPS both melt under polyester because of its
  styrene. Treat polyester and vinylester as unsafe on all polystyrene foam.
- **Superglue on foam board:** Wikipedia (Foamcore) says superglue makes the foam "melt away and
  dissolve" [citation needed]. The CA entry (137) and the market have "foam-safe CA". Both are right:
  ordinary CA attacks it, foam-safe CA doesn't.
- **Hot glue on foam:** foldnfly calls it the favourite for foam board. Studio Matrx says it is "too hot
  for thin foam". gluegun.com says standard-temperature hot glue "may melt" polystyrene and recommends
  low-temperature types.
- **Valley or V-cut depth in EVA:** Costumary says cut "halfway through"; "too deep" makes it fold instead
  of curve. Foodandcosplay removes a full 45° strip on each side for a sharp fold. These are different
  goals (curve vs corner), and entry 1 should say so.
- **Contact cement wait time:** Kamui says "Mine dries actually instantly". UHU por says 5-20 minutes.
  Costumary says wait until tacky. Follow the can and use the finger test.
- **Tape hinge limits:** Flite Test says tape hinges are "limited to electric planes made of foam" and
  "lose adhesion over time". Model Aviation says Blenderm suits models "under 8 ounces" and SR gapless
  tape suits 8-48 oz; tape is not fuel-proof.
- **Rubber-band wing mounts:** 3 bands each side; bands stretch about three times their length;
  discard after each session (fuel and UV) (Model Aviation).
- **Safety:** contact-cement fumes need "a proper half-face respirator with organic vapor cartridges"
  (Costumary). Neodymium magnets "can (and will) jump up to 8 inches", can cut or bruise fingers and
  shatter; pacemaker caution (whitearmor.net). Hot-wire cutting of EPS and XPS needs local exhaust
  ventilation and heat-resistant gloves (Harvard GSD Fab Lab SOP). Hot-glue nozzle burns (foldnfly). A
  Core77 poster suggests **gasoline** to lift foam-board paper: do not repeat this; it is a fire and
  health hazard. Epoxy exotherm can melt foam (Swaylocks). Superglue fumes and skin bonding (existing
  137).
