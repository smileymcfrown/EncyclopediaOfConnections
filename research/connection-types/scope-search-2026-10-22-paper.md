# Gap search: paper and cardboard (group `paper`), 2026-10-22

Scope: joints, hinges, fasteners and glues used with paper, card, paperboard and corrugated
cardboard. Pop-up mechanisms (V-fold, parallel fold, pull tab, volvelle, lift-the-flap) are left to
the pop-up agent; where my entries border them I say so. Working notes with every quote are kept in
`working-notes.md` in this folder (also appended at the end of this file).

## 1. Summary

- **9 new entries suggested** (ordered by usefulness): score-and-fold hinge; glued tab joint;
  adhesive tape joint and tape hinge; split-pin paper fastener; cardboard screws and rivets; glue
  stick and starch paste; folding box closures (tuck flaps, lock bottoms); sewn book bindings
  (pamphlet, Coptic and kettle stitch, Japanese stab binding); loose-leaf bindings (rings, coils,
  combs, discs).
- **24 existing entries to tag** with paper/card for the "Made of" filter (section 3), plus one (157) flagged as unconfirmed.
- **5 notes additions** to existing entries: staples 373 (desk stapler and saddle stapling),
  spring clip 398 (paper clip), contact adhesive 224 (spray adhesive), cross-halving 242 and
  tab-and-slot 239 (corrugated flute direction), snap-lock sheet joint 241 (cross-reference to box
  closures).
- **No new family proposed.** Reasons:
  1. The 30 families group entries by job or holding principle ("bends instead of hinging", "glues",
     "joints for sheet and panels"). A material family ("paper & card") would sit on a different
     axis. Every material group in this phase (foam, clay, composites, glass) would then claim the
     same right, and an entry such as hot glue or tab-and-slot would belong to five families at once.
  2. My 9 entries each fit an existing family well: 3 in adhesives & bonding, 2 in hinges & pivots,
     1 each in compliant mechanisms & flexures, interlocking joints & joinery, everyday fasteners &
     fixings, and knots, lashings & sewn joints. None is homeless.
  3. The new "Made of" filter already gives readers the paper-and-card view this would provide.
  4. If the owner still wants a front door for paper, a curated "Paper & card" collection page
     (filter preset) does the job without changing the scheme.
  5. The pop-up mechanisms are a different case. They are one coherent job: flat sheets that
     move or rise when a page opens or a tab is pulled. If the pop-up agent ends with 6 or more
     entries, a family such as `pop-ups & paper mechanisms` ("Rises or moves when a page opens")
     could be justified on the job axis. My first preference is still to put them in `motion
     conversion` (pull tabs, volvelles) and `compliant mechanisms & flexures` (V-folds, parallel
     folds, next to Origami mechanisms 178 and LET joint), with "Made of: paper".

## 4. Notes additions for existing entries

1. **373 Staples for wood and fabric.** Add desk (paper) stapling and consider renaming to
   "Staples (staple gun, desk stapler, hammer tacker, cable staple)". The entry now says tacking is
   "as opposed to 'stapling' paper with bent legs", so the commonest paper joint is left out. Add:
   the anvil folds the legs in (the "permanent" setting) or out (the "temporary" setting, easy to
   remove); flat-clinch staplers; saddle stapling through the fold of a booklet; long-reach staplers;
   stainless (Monel) staples for archival work. Sources: Wikipedia Stapler; Wikipedia Bookbinding
   ("Stapling through the centerfold, also called saddle-stitching"); Talas pamphlet stitch (staples
   "can rust, tear, or stain over time"; Monel staples); Make: (Bostitch and Rapid "sword-point"
   staplers for cardboard).
2. **398 Spring clip clamp.** Add the wire paper clip as the simplest spring clip. Wikipedia Paper
   clip: Gem type; it works by "torsion and elasticity in the wire, and friction between wire and
   paper". The binder clip is already there.
3. **224 Contact adhesive.** Add spray adhesive (spray mount) properly, since it is the go-to glue
   for mounting paper and card. 3M Super 77 data sheet: "Temporary and permanent lightweight bonds
   on substrates like papers, cardboard, fabrics, foams". Spraying one surface gives a short
   10-15 second open time for lighter, repositionable work; spraying both surfaces gives 1-30
   minutes and a permanent bond. Solvent and propellant are flammable, VOC 534 g/L, so use it
   outdoors or with ventilation and overspray protection.
4. **242 Cross-halving slot joint and 239 Tab-and-slot joint.** Add a corrugated-card note. Flute
   direction changes stiffness, and edge crush is "highly directional" (Wikipedia Corrugated
   fiberboard). Cut slots so the flutes run across the joint where possible. Panels are stronger
   "when the cardboard flutes are in different directions" (Makedo). In 239, replace the
   "[uncertain]" box-flap example with a link to the new Folding box closures entry.
5. **241 Snap-lock sheet joint.** Add a cross-reference to Folding box closures (tuck flaps and
   lock bottoms) so the tab-lock box alias points to the full treatment.

## 6. Safety points and figures that disagree

**Safety**
- Craft knives and scalpels for scoring, half-cuts and cutting card: cut away from the hand
  holding the ruler. Canon's guide repeats "Avoid cutting your fingers".
- Punches and awls: TinkerLab did a "quick safety review for the punch (to avoid punching
  directly into one's hand)". A school DT risk assessment says split pins can be sharp, so make
  holes first (search summary only; the Rob Ives page also says "Take care!" when piercing with a
  pencil).
- Spray adhesive and rubber cement: flammable solvents and propellant, high VOC (Super 77: 534 g/L)
  and inhalant abuse (Wikipedia Rubber cement). Use outdoors or with good ventilation. Acetone-based
  rubber cement damages plastics.
- Hot glue on cardboard: burns (already in entry 141).
- Small parts (cardboard screws, rivets, paper fasteners) and children: Makedo says under-3s
  should not have unsupervised access, 4+ with an adult, 6+ on their own.
- Wet paste and mould: starch paste is "a medium for molds, lichens, and fungus" (Wikipedia
  Corrugated box design, about box adhesive), so make it fresh.
- Staples rust and tear paper over time (Talas). Use stainless staples or sewing for things that
  must last.

**Figures and claims that disagree**
- Most common corrugated flute: Wikipedia says "C flute"; Adafruit says "'A' flute, which is 3/16"
  (4.8mm)". Wikipedia's table gives C as 4.0 mm and A as 4.8 mm. Prefer Wikipedia; ⚑.
- Cheaper tuck box: Sttark says the straight tuck takes "less paper", so it is cheaper. Diamond
  Packaging says the reverse tuck nests better and is "cost-effective", while the straight tuck
  uses "slightly higher board usage". ⚑ needs confirming; unimportant for hobbyists.
- Stab-binding hole distance from the spine: Wikipedia (traditional Chinese) "around 1 cm"; a
  Reddit comment "about 10-12mm"; Dorset History Centre "2cm in from the edge"; Talas spaces holes
  1 in apart along the spine but gives no inset. Treat as a range of 1-2 cm, depending on book size.
- Thread length: Talas pamphlet stitch "4-5 times the height"; Talas stab binding "five times the
  height"; Dorset "5 x the width of the paper" (for a 4-hole stab binding along a short edge).
- Pamphlet stitch end holes: Talas "about 1 cm from the top"; a search summary said about an inch
  (page not opened).
- Which way to fold a crease: forum advice disagrees (score side in or out). A Holmen paperboard
  guide reportedly says fold towards the crease bead and never turn the crease inside out, but the
  page was blocked (403) and was not read: ⚑ needs confirming.
