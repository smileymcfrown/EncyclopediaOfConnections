A pair of strips, one with thousands of tiny springy hooks and one with a pile of soft loops, that cling together wherever they are pressed and can be peeled apart and closed again thousands of times.

**In plain words:** Two fabric strips, one covered in tiny hooks and one in soft fuzzy loops, that grab each other when you press them together and rip apart when you [peel](../glossary.md#peel-vs-shear) them. **Proper term:** Hook-and-loop fastener (touch fastener).

**Also called:** Velcro (brand name of Velcro Companies, widely used as a generic name); hook-and-pile fastener; touch fastener (the term used in ASTM test standards); hook and loop tape / hook and loop strips, dots or coins; sew-on hook and loop; adhesive-backed or sticky-back hook and loop; back-to-back hook and loop / wrap strap / cable-tie strap; ONE-WRAP (Velcro brand); Scotchmate (3M brand); mushroom-head reclosable fastener / snap-together fastener (the plastic-stem variant); Dual Lock (3M brand), Duotec (brand); Quiet Closures (silent version); Klettband / Klettverschluss (German); Metaklett (steel version).

## How it works

Pressing the two strips together pushes thousands of small hooks into the loop pile, and each hook catches a loop at random (there is no need to line them up). A single hook is weak, but spread over a large area they add up. Sliding the strips along each other (shear) loads many hooks at once, and hooks that slip can catch new loops, so it is strongest that way; pulling straight apart is weaker; and peeling from one edge loads only one row of hooks at a time, so it opens easily, with the typical ripping sound. The hooks bend open to let go and spring back, so it can be closed again many times.

## Parts

A few parts: two mating tapes. The hook tape carries tiny springy hooks (woven nylon loops cut open into J-shaped hooks, or moulded plastic hooks); the loop tape carries a pile of soft loops (woven, knitted or non-woven). Each tape has a backing that is sewn on, stuck on with pressure-sensitive adhesive, or screwed, riveted or moulded on. Variants: back-to-back straps with hooks on one face and loops on the other, and 'mushroom' tapes with plastic stems whose caps snap into each other (both halves identical).

## Everyday examples

- children's shoes and trainers with hook-and-loop straps
- reusable cable ties and cord wraps
- orthopaedic braces, bag closures and jacket pocket flaps
- guitar pedals held onto a pedalboard

## When to use it

When you want a join you can open and close by hand, quickly and often, without lining anything up: pockets, flaps, straps that need adjusting, wrapping cables, holding a remote, battery pack or controller onto a surface, removable covers and cushions, and light panels or trim. It suits fabric and flexible parts, fixes at any position, is quiet to fit (no holes needed with adhesive-backed tape) and gives way gently if overloaded.

## When not to use it

Not for heavy or safety-critical loads, or where the join is pulled in peel (use a buckle, snap, clip or screws). Not where it must locate parts precisely (use pins or a keyed fit). Not in dusty, hairy or linty places or near knitwear, where the hooks clog or snag (use magnets, press studs or a zipper). Not for sealing (use a zipper with a sealed tape, or a gasket). For rigid parts that need a firm, rattle-free, click-shut join, a mushroom-head fastener (Dual Lock type) or magnets are better; for high temperatures use screws or a metal fastening.

## Details

- **What it does (jobs):**
  - join and hold: holds fabric, foam, straps and light panels or objects together or onto a surface
  - lock, and release by hand: press to close, peel to open, with no tools
  - tighten and hold tension: a strap can be pulled to any length and pressed down (cable wraps, shoe straps, braces)
- **Movement:** none: it is a holding joint; the backings may bend, and opening is a peel
- **Directions it can move (degrees of freedom):** 0 once pressed together (the two strips can't slide or lift while engaged); before closing, the strips can be placed anywhere over each other, so position is set at closing time rather than by the part
- **Special behaviour:**
  - quick-release (peels open by hand and closes again with a press)
  - tensioning (on a strap, it holds at whatever length you pull it to; a strap wrapped through a slot and back halves the force on the strip, like a pulley, and puts the load in shear; Wikipedia)
  - [fail-safe](../glossary.md#fail-safe--fail-secure) or breakaway (under overload, especially a peel, it lets go instead of tearing the parts; tag rugby and flag football rely on this)
- **How it holds:** shape (the hooks catch the loops; [form-fit](../glossary.md#form-fit--force-fit--material-bond)), with spring force (the hooks and, in mushroom types, the stems flex to engage and spring back); the tapes themselves are fixed by sewing, adhesive (material bond) or screws
- **Input and output:** not applicable
- **Power:** passive (no power needed)
- **Load:** light: roughly 1 to 20 lb per square inch (about 0.07 to 1.4 kg per square centimetre) depending on direction, type and whether the load is brief or constant. Strong in sliding (shear), medium when pulled straight apart (tension or cleavage between rigid parts), weak in peel. Example for 3M nylon hook and loop: 15 lb/in² dynamic shear, 9 lb/in² straight pull apart, but only 1.0 lb per inch of width in a flexible-to-flexible peel (3M SJ3403/SJ3404). Mushroom-head types are much stronger in tension (about 43 lb/in² for 3M Dual Lock 250/250).
- **Play and precision:** not applicable as a locator: it holds wherever it is pressed, so the parts don't return to exactly the same place; it tolerates large [misalignment](../glossary.md#misalignment) (any overlap engages); a soft pile has some give, so parts can shift slightly under load
- **Taking it apart:** tool-free quick release: peels apart by hand; the tapes themselves are semi-permanent (sewn or glued on)
- **How often it can be reused:** many times: 3M rates nylon hook to nylon loop at 5,000 closures before it loses half its peel strength; mushroom-head (Dual Lock) types are rated at 1,000 cycles to half strength (3M data sheets)
- **Sealing:** none: it is open fabric and lets air, water and dust through
- **Adjustable:** yes: it holds wherever the pieces overlap, so position and strap tension can be changed each time it is closed; you can also add or remove area to change strength
- **Access needed:** one side only (blind): each half is fixed to its own part, and it closes with a press and opens with a peel from the outside; adhesive-backed pieces can be stuck onto a surface you can only reach from the front
- **Materials:** Usually woven nylon hooks and loops (longest cycle life); polyester for outdoor and wet use (better UV resistance, works when wet, shorter cycle life; Halco); polypropylene low-profile hook on back-to-back straps; polyolefin stems on mushroom types (3M Dual Lock); special aerospace tapes with PTFE (Teflon) loops, polyester hooks and glass backing (Wikipedia); a steel version exists (Metaklett). Sew-on tape suits fabric, webbing and leather; adhesive-backed tape (for example with acrylic adhesive) needs clean, dry, smooth surfaces such as most rigid plastics, metal, glass, painted wood and 3D prints; highly textured, rough or porous surfaces, or surfaces with silicone mould release, may need light abrading, an adhesion promoter or a sealer, or a mechanical fixing (3M SJ60L data sheet).
- **Heat, cold, water and wear:** Heat: adhesive-backed nylon tape is rated for long-term use to about 82 °C and Dual Lock to 93 °C (3M data sheets); adhesive tape can [creep](../glossary.md#creep) or let go in a hot car ⚑ [uncertain: general experience, no test opened]. Water: nylon absorbs water and doesn't fasten as well when wet; polyester works when wet (Halco). Sunlight: nylon withstands UV less well than polyester, so use polyester outdoors (Halco). Dirt: lint, hair and dust build up in the hooks over months and weaken it (Wikipedia). Wear: loops stretch or break with long use (Wikipedia). 3M's nylon pair held a static load for 10,000 minutes at 100 °F / 100% humidity without failing. Printed PETG versions share PETG's heat limit.

## Making it

- **Ways to make or get it:**
  - off-the-shelf part: the usual choice (sew-on or adhesive-backed tape, dots, coins, straps and cable wraps)
  - sewing (textiles): sew-on tape stitched onto fabric, webbing or leather
  - 3D print (FDM): fully printed hook-and-loop-style pads (small posts that snag each other) in PETG, or printed parts with slots or flat pads for bought straps and tape
  - woodwork, laser cut, CNC and sheet metal: make the rigid parts that adhesive-backed or screw-on tape is fixed to
- **Printing it in one piece (print in place):** no: printed hook-and-loop is two separate printed pads (each prints complete in one piece, without supports) that are pressed together after printing
- **Tools:** To use: none. To fit: scissors to cut it to length, plus a sewing machine (or needle and thread) for sew-on tape, or a clean surface, isopropyl alcohol and a roller for adhesive-backed tape; screws or rivets for some rigid types.
- **Difficulty:** easy: buy tape, cut it with scissors, then sew it, stick it or screw it on; printing your own hook-and-loop is moderate (it needs good layer adhesion and fine settings)

### 3D printing tips

Attaching bought tape to prints: use adhesive-backed tape on a clean, flat, smooth face (wipe with isopropyl alcohol), apply with firm pressure (3M suggests about 310 g/cm² or 4.5 psi, e.g. with a roller) with parts at 20-38 °C, round the corners, roll the edges down, and recess the tape slightly or add a raised rim so the edges can't lift (3M SJ60L data sheet). Highly textured surfaces reduce adhesion (3M), so stick the tape to the smoothest face of the print, such as the face printed against the bed. For straps, design a slot the strap can loop through so the load is in sliding (shear) instead of peel. Printing the fastener itself: the best-tested designs use rows of tiny posts or towers with overhanging heads. Teaching Tech's parametric OpenSCAD model (a remix of MM Printing's and eried's designs) recommends PETG at 0.28-0.3 mm layers; a little stringing actually helps the halves grip, and interlayer strength must be good or the tiny towers snap off. Keep pads that must peel thin and flexible. In Teaching Tech's tests TPU didn't lock well and PETG worked best (Hackaday, 2023); MM Printing advises against TPU unless the base is PLA, PETG or ABS, and prints with no supports, 0.3 mm layers and 100% [infill](../glossary.md#infill). Because the printed pattern is regular rather than random, the halves need lining up more carefully than fabric hook-and-loop. For flexible printed straps, soft PLA or TPU straps with printed hooks that catch a mesh also work (ElastoStraps, Nothing Labs).

### Buying one (off-the-shelf parts)

Hook and loop tape (sew-on / plain back; adhesive-backed with rubber or acrylic adhesive), hook and loop dots and coins, back-to-back hook and loop (cable wraps and ties, e.g. ONE-WRAP), hook and loop straps with buckles or D-rings, industrial reclosable fasteners (3M Scotchmate), mushroom-head reclosable fasteners (3M Dual Lock, in stem densities of about 170, 250 and 400 per square inch), flame-resistant, high-temperature and chemical-resistant grades, and polyester (outdoor/marine) grades. McMaster-Carr lists them as 'Hook and Loop', 'Hook and Loop Straps' and 'Snap-Together Fasteners' (mushroom heads). Search terms: 'hook and loop tape sew-on', 'adhesive back hook and loop', 'back-to-back hook and loop', 'reclosable fastener', 'mushroom head fastener'.

## Rules of thumb

- Design so the load slides the strips along each other (shear), not so it peels them from an edge: typical 3M nylon hook and loop takes about 15 lb/in² in dynamic shear and 9 lb/in² pulled straight apart between rigid parts, but only 1.0 lb per inch of width in a flexible-to-flexible 'T' peel, 2.7 lb/in in a 90° peel and 9.0 lb/in in cleavage between rigid parts (3M SJ3403/SJ3404 data sheet, https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/796373O/hook-and-loop-fasteners-sj3403-and-sj3404.pdf).
- For a long-term hanging load, count on much less than the dynamic figures: the same 3M pair held 2.2 lb/in² in static shear and 1.1 lb/in² in static tension for 10,000 minutes without failing, at room temperature and at 100 °F / 100% humidity (same 3M data sheet).
- Three ways to make a flexible join stronger: more area, force parallel to the strip (for example bending it round a corner or pulley), and more hooks and loops per unit area (Wikipedia, 'Velcro', Strength section, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velcro).
- Expect about 5,000 open-close cycles for nylon hook to nylon loop before peel strength halves (3M SJ60L data sheet, https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/2366389O/3m-loop-fastener-tape-sj60l.pdf) and about 1,000 for mushroom-head Dual Lock (3M SJ3550 data sheet, https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/2366370O/3M-Dual-Lock-Reclosable-Fastener-SJ3550.pdf).
- Adhesive-backed nylon tape is rated for long-term use up to about 82 °C (180 °F) (3M SJ60L); apply at about 310 g/cm² (4.5 psi) to clean surfaces between 20 and 38 °C, and round corners or roll edges down to stop edge lifting (same 3M data sheet).
- For mushroom-head Dual Lock, don't pair the lowest density with itself (too weak) or the highest with itself (so strong that stems and heads can rip out); 250 to 250 is the usual pairing (3M SJ3550 data sheet).
- Sew-on tape: use a 14/90 to 16/100 needle, polyester thread, 8-10 stitches per inch and a box stitch round all edges about 1/16 inch in; never sew adhesive-backed tape, as the glue gums up the needle (Velcro sew-on guide on iTapeStore, https://www.itapestore.com/sewing-guide-velcro-brand-hook-loop-sew-on/; Brother warns that thin 65/9-75/11 needles may bend or break, https://support.brother.com/g/b/faqend.aspx?c=us_ot&lang=en&prod=hf_cp100xeus&faqid=faqh00101524_000).
- Printed hook-and-loop: PETG at 0.28-0.3 mm layer height, with good interlayer strength (Teaching Tech, Printables model 568587, read via the Printables API).

## Common failures

(1) Loaded in peel instead of shear, so it unzips from one edge: route straps through a slot or round a corner, or use a bigger area. (2) Hooks clogged with lint and hair: clean them with a stiff brush or another piece of hook, and close it before washing (Velcro care advice). (3) Loops worn flat or stretched after long use: replace the loop side (Wikipedia). (4) Adhesive backing peeling off, especially at corners, on rough or dirty surfaces or in heat: clean with isopropyl alcohol, press hard, round the corners, recess the tape or add screws or rivets at the corners (3M). (5) Sewing adhesive-backed tape gums up the machine needle: buy sew-on tape (Brother). (6) Printed hooks snapping off because of weak layer adhesion, or TPU versions not locking: print in PETG with well-tuned settings (Teaching Tech; MM Printing).

## Safety

Nylon hook and loop burns: the Apollo 1 investigation cited almost 34 square feet of Velcro near the ignition source as part of the flammable material in the cabin fire (Wikipedia); use flame-resistant grades near heat or in oxygen-rich settings (McMaster-Carr lists flame-resistant types). The hook side snags hair, knitwear and loosely woven fabric, which can be damaged when pulled off (Wikipedia; Velcro advises keeping the hook side covered). Don't rely on it to hold anything that could fall and hurt someone.

## Related

- Mushroom-head reclosable fastener (3M Dual Lock type): plastic stems with mushroom caps that snap together with an audible click; much stronger and more rattle-free on rigid parts, but fewer cycles (about 1,000)
- Zipper (slide fastener): closes a long opening edge to edge in one pull and resists peeling, but isn't adjustable in position
- Press stud (snap fastener): holds at fixed points only, doesn't clog with lint, and survives washing well
- Side-release buckle and ladder-lock buckle: for straps that carry real loads or must stay shut when pulled
- Magnet joint (embedded magnets, magnetic catch): for rigid parts that should snap into the same position every time, with no fibres to clog
- Double-sided foam tape: a near-permanent sticky bond instead of a reclosable one
- Hook-and-eye closure: the full-size ancestor, one hook to each eye, placed one by one (Wikipedia)
- Metaklett: steel hook-and-loop sheets for hot or heavily loaded joints; its 35 tonnes per square metre at 800 °C claim is unreferenced on Wikipedia ⚑ [uncertain]
