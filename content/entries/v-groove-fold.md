Cutting a V-shaped (or square) groove almost through the back of a sheet leaves a thin uncut face that acts as a hinge, so the sheet can be folded until the two cut faces of the groove meet, giving a crisp, seamless corner that is then glued or fixed.

**In plain words:** A V-shaped strip cut out of the back of a sheet, almost all the way through, so the thin face left behind bends like a hinge and the sheet folds up into a neat corner that is then glued. **Proper term:** V-groove fold (mitre fold, 'miterfold'; routing-and-folding method).

**Also called:** mitre fold, miterfold, miter fold (woodweb Knowledge Base 'Miterfolds for clean corner joints'); V-groove and fold, V-grooving, routing and folding method (Alucobond, for aluminium composite panels; Vitracore G2 machining guidelines); fold-up corner, folded box from a grooved panel (Fine Woodworking plywood trays; Roy Underhill's hand method); beveled cut fold, bevelled fold (Fold 'N Fly, foam board); overlap fold, square-groove fold (Fold 'N Fly): two parallel cuts one board-thickness apart with the foam strip peeled out, so the paper skin wraps the corner; A-style fold and B-style fold, also 'A fold', 'B fold' (Flite Test foam-board plane plans); folded edges (EVA foam armour; Food and Cosplay); rabbet or rebate fold in foam core (Core77 forum; a one-piece version of the rabbet joint); Auto V groover, Betterley (brands of V-grooving machines mentioned on woodweb).

## How it works

The groove removes the material that would otherwise be crushed on the inside of the bend, and leaves only a thin outer layer, which bends easily because bending [stiffness](../glossary.md#stiffness) falls steeply with thickness. A 90° V-groove (two 45° bevels) closes up exactly when the sheet is folded to 90°, so the two cut faces meet face to face and make a glue joint, while the uncut outer skin wraps the corner without a visible seam (Fold 'N Fly: 'when you fold the foam board into a 90° angle, it will fit together perfectly and the remaining paper layer will make a perfect, clean corner'). The groove angle sets the fold angle: Alucobond uses a 90° V-groove for folds up to 90°, a 135° V-groove for folds up to 135°, and a rectangular groove for folds up to 150° (Alucobond). In a square-groove ('overlap') fold in foam board, a strip of foam one board-thickness wide is peeled out so the paper skin wraps round the end of the other side (Fold 'N Fly). If the groove is left unglued, the thin skin stays a hinge, which is how foam-board planes make their control-surface hinges (Model Aviation 'PAP-E': 'don't go through the last layer of paper, because it forms the hinge').

## Parts

Single piece: one sheet with a groove cut in its back, plus glue (and, in wood, tape or a band clamp to hold it while the glue sets). The parts of the joint are the two bevelled (or square) groove walls, the thin uncut skin at the bottom of the groove that becomes the outside corner, and the glue line (Fold 'N Fly; Alucobond; Fine Woodworking).

## Everyday examples

- The square box fuselages of foam-board model planes, such as Flite Test scratch builds (Flite Test, Fowl Flyer and Old Fogey)
- Sharp ridges and folded edges on EVA foam cosplay armour without a visible seam (Food and Cosplay)
- Folded aluminium composite sign trays, fascia, wall cladding and column covers on shop fronts (Alucobond)
- Kitchen worktops and laminate tops with a seamless 'miterfolded' front edge and splashback, and small folded plywood trays (woodweb; Fine Woodworking)

## When to use it

Use a V-groove fold when you want a clean, seamless outside corner in sheet material and want the parts to line themselves up: the uncut skin keeps the sides in register while you glue, and the outside face (paper, veneer, laminate or metal) continues unbroken round the corner. It is the standard way to build boxes and fuselages from foam board (Fold 'N Fly; Flite Test), to make sharp folded edges in EVA foam armour (Food and Cosplay), to fold aluminium composite sign trays and cladding on site with a hand router (Alucobond), and to make quick folded plywood trays or veneered boxes with matched grain round the corners (Fine Woodworking; woodweb). It also saves cutting and aligning separate side pieces.

## When not to use it

Avoid it for joints that must be taken apart: it is permanent once glued; use tab-and-slot, screws or hook-and-loop instead. Avoid it where the corner is pulled open or levered often, because the skin and glue line are weak in [peel](../glossary.md#peel-vs-shear); add a glued strip, an L angle or a [fillet](../glossary.md#chamfer--fillet) inside (Vitracore suggests an L angle when the groove is cut too deep) ⚑ [uncertain]. For a hinge that flexes many times, use a tape hinge, a slot-in model hinge or a [living hinge](../glossary.md#living-hinge) rather than a bare foam-board skin. For curves rather than a single sharp fold, use [kerf](../glossary.md#kerf) bending (valley cuts) instead. For two separate pieces meeting at a corner, use a mitre, butt or rebate (rabbet) joint. Not for fire-rated ALUCOBOND A2 panels (Alucobond).

## Details

- **What it does (jobs):**
  - join and hold: makes a strong, square corner in one piece, without a separate joint, once the groove is glued
  - glue or bond: the glued groove faces hold the angle (hot glue, contact cement, PVA, polyurethane glue or epoxy depending on material)
  - let it move (turn, slide or bend): left unglued, the thin skin is a hinge, for example the control surfaces of foam-board planes (Model Aviation 'PAP-E') and the folds that shape a Flite Test fuselage before gluing
- **Movement:** flex: during assembly the thin skin bends through the fold angle, usually 90° (up to 90°, 135° or about 150° depending on the groove shape; Alucobond). none once glued. Left unglued as a hinge, it swings back and forth through the angle the groove allows ⚑ [uncertain for limits].
- **Directions it can move (degrees of freedom):** 1 while folding (rotation about the line of the groove), 0 once glued. As an unglued hinge it has 1, plus some unwanted sideways give because the skin is thin and soft.
- **Special behaviour:**
  - self-aligning or self-centring: the fold line is set by the groove, so the corner lands in the right place every time; Flite Test's 'B-style' fold 'will allow for a self-squaring assembly' (Flite Test, Fowl Flyer)
  - none otherwise: once glued it is a rigid corner
- **How it holds:**
  - [material bond](../glossary.md#form-fit--force-fit--material-bond) (glue, solvent or melting): the groove faces are glued (hot glue in foam board, Fold 'N Fly; contact cement or glue in EVA, Food and Cosplay; PVA or other wood glue in plywood and veneer, woodweb and Fine Woodworking)
  - shape (the parts interlock; form-fit): the uncut skin ties the two sides together, and in a square-groove fold one side rests on the other
  - not a holding joint (it moves or passes on motion): when left unglued as a flexing hinge
- **Input and output:** not applicable
- **Power:** passive (no power needed)
- **Load:** light to medium, depending on material. In foam board, a beveled or overlap fold with hot glue inside gives 'a very sturdy joint' (Fold 'N Fly). In plywood the glue joint is between end-grain bevels, which a Fine Woodworking reader questioned for strength ('100% [end grain](../glossary.md#end-grain) glue joints') ⚑ [uncertain]. It resists bending that closes the corner, but pulling it open puts the thin skin and glue line in peel, its weak direction ⚑ [uncertain]. Aluminium composite folds are used for fascia, wall cladding and column covers (Alucobond); if the groove cuts into the face skin, Vitracore suggests gluing an L angle down the back of the fold.
- **Play and precision:** No play once glued. [Accuracy](../glossary.md#repeatability--accuracy) depends on cutting the groove at a constant depth and on the line: Fold 'N Fly notes that with a score-and-fold or beveled fold the measured line is the inside or outside dimension, and the other side is about 5 mm (one board thickness) different; with the overlap fold either cut line can be the outer dimension. In plywood, keep using panels from the same sheet once the depth is set, because thickness varies (Fine Woodworking; woodweb). Woodweb's fabricators groove from the face down so the remaining skin is always the same.
- **Taking it apart:** permanent: the skin and glue line must be cut to separate it; aluminium composite folds are also permanent once bent ⚑ [uncertain]
- **How often it can be reused:** once as a glued corner. As an unglued hinge in foam board, a moderate number of flexes before the paper skin tears; Flite Test spreads hot glue into the crease to 'create a rubber-like hinge and help keep your paper from tearing' ⚑ [uncertain: no cycle counts found]. Aluminium composite panels are folded once ⚑ [uncertain whether they can be refolded].
- **Sealing:** none as a joint in foam or card, though a glued groove with a continuous outer skin has no gap at the corner; in aluminium composite the unbroken outer metal skin keeps rain off at the fold ⚑ [uncertain]
- **Adjustable:** no: the fold angle is set by the groove and fixed by the glue. A shallower or steeper groove gives a different angle (Alucobond).
- **Access needed:** both sides: the groove is cut from the back and the fold is checked from the face; for veneered panels the face is taped before the groove is cut through from the back (woodweb)
- **Materials:** Suits sheets with a tough face layer or a tough, bendable core: paper-faced foam board (Fold 'N Fly; Flite Test; Core77), EVA foam, where the foam left at the bottom of the groove is the hinge (Food and Cosplay), plywood and MDF (Fine Woodworking), wood veneer, laminate and solid-surface tops (woodweb), and aluminium composite panels (Alucobond, Vitracore). [Brittle](../glossary.md#brittle--ductile) materials and solid surface are cut right through and held by tape on the face instead (woodweb). Not suitable for fire-rated ALUCOBOND A2 (Alucobond). Glue choice depends on the material: foam board and polystyrene foams need foam-safe glues (hot glue, PVA, epoxy or foam-safe CA), because ordinary super glue and solvent glues melt polystyrene (Fold 'N Fly; Studio Matrx; Model Aviation 'PAP-E'); EVA foam is usually joined with contact cement (Costumary). Card and cardboard can also be folded this way ⚑ [uncertain: Fold 'N Fly says cardboard can substitute for foam board, but no source showed V-grooved card].
- **Heat, cold, water and wear:** Hot glue softens in heat ('melts in heat', Costumary), so foam-board and EVA folds glued with it can let go in a hot car or in sun ⚑ [uncertain for temperatures]. Paper-faced foam board is weakened by damp, while plastic-faced and waterproof foam boards resist it (Fold 'N Fly mentions plastic coatings 'which can make it more durable and waterproof'). Solvent-based glues and paints melt polystyrene foam board (Fold 'N Fly; Studio Matrx). Aluminium composite folds are made for outdoor cladding (Alucobond). Wood and plywood corners move with humidity like any glued wood joint ⚑ [uncertain].

## Making it

- **Ways to make or get it:**
  - hand cut: foam board, EVA foam and card with a knife and metal ruler (Fold 'N Fly; Food and Cosplay)
  - woodwork: router with a V-groove bit, or table saw with the blade at 45° (Fine Woodworking; woodweb)
  - CNC: V-groovers and CNC routers for aluminium composite panels and production miterfolding (Alucobond; Vitracore; woodweb)
  - 3D print (FDM): a flat print with V-shaped grooves and a thin skin of one or two layers can be folded up, like printed origami models ⚑ [uncertain for strength]
- **Printing it in one piece (print in place):** partly: a part can be printed flat with V-grooves leaving a thin flexible skin and folded up after printing, the same idea as printed 'origami' models with thin living-hinge layers (Printables 'Origami Valentine's Day Heart'), but folding and gluing are needed afterwards ⚑ [uncertain]
- **Tools:** Foam board and EVA foam: a sharp hobby knife or snap-off utility knife, a metal ruler and a cutting mat (Fold 'N Fly; Costumary), optionally a two-blade cutter for square grooves (Printables 'Foam Board Cutter - 3/16" Gap') or a rabbet cutter (Core77), and a glue gun or contact cement. Plywood and MDF: a router with a V-groove bit and fence (Fine Woodworking), or a table saw with the blade tilted to 45° (woodweb), plus glue, packing or blue tape and a band clamp. Aluminium composite: a router or CNC with a 90° V-groover with a 3 mm flat, or a panel saw with grooving accessories (Alucobond; Vitracore G2).
- **Difficulty:** moderate: easy in foam board and EVA with a sharp knife and a metal ruler, but the cut depth must be controlled so you don't cut through the skin (Fold 'N Fly's three-pass cutting method helps); in plywood it needs a router or table saw and test cuts to '[creep](../glossary.md#creep) up to the perfect depth' (Fine Woodworking)

### 3D printing tips

1) Print the part flat on the bed with the grooves facing up and the thin skin on the bed; the skin becomes the outside of the corner ⚑ [uncertain]. 2) Make the skin from a small number of layers: printed origami models from the same idea use a 0.2 mm hinge printed as two 0.1 mm layers that run in opposite directions, with the [infill](../glossary.md#infill) angle set so the filament runs across the fold line, and say 'PETG will likely yield more robust results, due to its greater flexibility' than PLA (Printables models 1190897 and 1473362). 3) Use a 90° V-groove for a 90° corner so the groove walls close flat for gluing, as in foam board (Fold 'N Fly) and composite panels (Alucobond). 4) Fold while the part is warm or right after printing, and bias the hinges gently before the first full fold (Printables model 1190897 links a video on how to 'bias the hinges') ⚑ [uncertain]. 5) Glue the closed groove with CA or epoxy if the corner must be rigid. 6) For a part that must fold many times, use a proper living hinge in polypropylene or PETG instead (see Living hinge).

### Buying one (off-the-shelf parts)

Foam board (paper-faced polystyrene, usually about 5 mm thick, Fold 'N Fly; a builder's comment on Flite Test's Old Fogey article mentions a speed-build kit 'made out of waterproof foam board'); EVA foam sheet (2, 6 and 10 mm are common for cosplay; Costumary); V-groove router bits and 90° folding V-groove cutters for aluminium composite (Vitracore: '90 degree V-Groover with a 3mm flat'); foam-board rabbet cutters, e.g. Logan FoamWerks (brand) (Core77); two-blade foam-board cutters that use hobby knife blades; low-temperature glue guns (Fold 'N Fly); contact cement, e.g. Barge or DAP Weldwood (brands) (Costumary); clear packing tape and fibre-reinforced tape (Fold 'N Fly); blue painter's tape and a band clamp (Fine Woodworking).

## Rules of thumb

- Foam board score-and-fold depth: 'cut through the first layer of paper and some of the foam, but not into the bottom layer of paper'; after folding, the outside dimension is about 5 mm (one board thickness) more than the line measured (Fold 'N Fly).
- Foam board beveled fold: make a 45° cut on each side of the fold line without cutting into the bottom paper, so the two bevels close into a 90° corner; put a bead of hot glue in the joint and hold it at the right angle for a few seconds. The measured line is now the outside dimension, and the inside is about 5 mm less (Fold 'N Fly).
- Foam board overlap (square-groove) fold: two parallel cuts that leave the back paper intact, 'The distance between the two cuts should be the thickness of the foam board, usually 4-5mm'; peel the foam strip out, fold, and hot glue (Fold 'N Fly). A two-blade cutter for Flite Test plans holds two #11 blades 3/16 in apart and cuts 3/16 in deep, because 'their plans have you cut long straight edges exactly to this gap, so that the foam board will fold over perfectly' (Printables model 412271). A Core77 poster cuts 'two slits almost all the way through to the second sheet, 1/8" apart' and removes the foam.
- Cut foam board in several light passes: first the top paper, then the foam, then (for a through cut) the bottom paper; this teaches you how deep each cut is (Fold 'N Fly).
- Flite Test folds: the 'A-style' fold 'is designed to set the side walls on top of the bottom', while the 'B-style' fold 'simply folds up where the side wall is against the bottom' and gives a self-squaring fuselage; check the plans for which to use (Flite Test, Fowl Flyer Swappable). Spreading hot glue into the foam of a crease 'will create a rubber-like hinge and help keep your paper from tearing' (Flite Test, Old Fogey).
- Foam-board hinge left unglued: 'Make two parallel 3/8-inch cuts through the foam board, approximately 1/8-inch apart. Cut at an angle, but don't go through the last layer of paper, because it forms the hinge' (Model Aviation, 'PAP-E', 2000).
- EVA foam folded edge: draw the fold line on the underside, 'cut out a strip of the foam on either side at a 45 degree angle of the fold line', glue all the way down the gap, fold and hold until dry (Food and Cosplay, 2015). With contact cement, coat both surfaces, wait 3-5 minutes until tacky, then press; let it [cure](../glossary.md#cure) at least 30 minutes before stressing the joint; coating only one side gives 'maybe 30% of the bond strength' (Costumary).
- EVA curves are different: valley cuts halfway through the thickness, spaced 2-3 cm apart, bend the foam into a curve; 'Don't cut too deep or the foam will fold instead of curve' (Costumary). That belongs to kerf bending.
- Aluminium composite: rout a V-shaped or rectangular groove on the back, leaving 'a thin layer of the core material' on the inside of the outer skin; 90° V-groove for folds up to 90°, 135° V-groove up to 135°, rectangular groove up to 150° depending on panel thickness (Alucobond). 'Traditionally solid core ACP is grooved leaving approximately 0.3mm of core material remaining', using a 90° V-groover with a 3 mm flat; keep tools sharp, because blunt tooling raises heat and pressure and spoils the groove (Vitracore G2 Machining Guidelines v1.2).
- Plywood trays (Fine Woodworking): size the panel as the tray length and width plus the side height added to all four edges; set the router fence so the distance to the point of the V-bit equals the side height; creep up to the depth in several passes 'for easy folding without breaking'; cover the back of the grooves with blue tape before folding to avoid breakage; glue the grooves and hold with tape and a band clamp. Roy Underhill's hand method cuts the V 'just short of breaking through the opposite side'.
- Wood and veneer miterfold with tape (woodweb): cut 45° bevels with the face up on a table saw, put the edges together and tape the face with heavy packing tape, flip, glue, fold; don't use tape so aggressive that it pulls the face veneer off; for brittle materials cut right through 'and just slightly into the tape'.

## Common failures

1) Cutting through the skin, so the fold breaks apart: use several light passes (Fold 'N Fly), set the depth on scrap and creep up to it (Fine Woodworking), and tape the back of the groove before folding (Fine Woodworking); in brittle materials cut through on purpose and use tape as the hinge (woodweb). 2) Not cutting deep enough, so the fold won't close or the foam crushes and bulges at the inside corner: remove the full V or strip of foam (Fold 'N Fly). 3) A stair-stepped, rough corner from a plain score-and-fold: switch to a beveled or overlap fold (Fold 'N Fly). 4) The paper skin tearing at a repeatedly flexed foam-board hinge: spread hot glue into the crease or tape it (Flite Test, Old Fogey) ⚑ [uncertain for life]. 5) Glue melting the foam: use foam-safe glue, not ordinary super glue or solvent glue (Fold 'N Fly; Studio Matrx). 6) Weak EVA seam from pressing contact cement while still wet or coating only one side (Costumary). 7) Packing tape pulling veneer off when removed (woodweb). 8) Grooves of varying depth because the plywood thickness varies or the panel isn't flat (woodweb; Vitracore 'Groove on a flat even surface to ensure depth'). 9) Corners that are not square because the fold lines were measured to the wrong side; remember the board-thickness offset (Fold 'N Fly).

## Safety

Sharp knives: use a sharp blade (blunt blades tear and need more force), keep the cap on when not cutting, cut on a mat, never on your lap, and keep fingers out of the path of a slipping blade; children should be supervised (Fold 'N Fly). Router bits and saws: V-grooving bits get very hot and dull fast (woodweb); follow the tool maker's guarding and push-stick advice and support the panel. Contact cement: use it only with good ventilation and, indoors, 'a proper half-face respirator with organic vapor cartridges', not a dust mask (Costumary); spray adhesives also give off harmful fumes (Fold 'N Fly). Hot glue: the nozzle and glue cause serious burns, and a gun left plugged in is a fire risk; low-temperature guns are kinder to foam (Fold 'N Fly). Heat guns used to shape EVA can burn the foam if held in one place (Food and Cosplay). Never use petrol or other solvents to lift foam-board paper, a suggestion seen on a forum (Core77).

## Related

- Kerf bending: many partial cuts that bend a sheet into a curve, not one groove that folds it into a sharp corner (valley cuts in EVA belong there)
- Score-and-fold hinge (scored crease): a single cut or crease in card or foam board with no material removed; the V-groove fold removes a wedge so the corner closes neatly
- Living hinge: a moulded or printed thin web meant to flex many times; a V-groove skin is usually glued fixed
- Butt and lap wood joints (incl. mitre butt): join two separate pieces; a V-groove fold is a mitre joint made from one piece with the outer face left joined
- Housing and rebate (rabbet) joints: the two-piece version of the foam-board overlap fold (Core77 rabbet cutters)
- Glued joint design: covers the bevelled (mitred) seam between two separate EVA pieces
- Contact adhesive and hot-melt adhesive: the usual glues for EVA and foam-board folds
- Adhesive tape joint and tape hinge: tape across a seam or used as the hinge of a mitre fold in brittle materials
- Origami mechanisms (thick-panel origami): folding patterns in thick panels, where V-grooves or gaps make room for the panel thickness
