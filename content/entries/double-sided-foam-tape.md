A roll of tape made of a soft foam layer with pressure-sensitive glue on both faces, which sticks two parts together as soon as you press them, spreads the load along the whole joint, soaks up small gaps and vibration, and can also seal the joint.

**In plain words:** A thick, squashy sticky tape with glue on both sides that you press between two parts to stick them together for good, with no drilling, screws or waiting for glue to dry. **Proper term:** Double-sided foam tape (acrylic foam pressure-sensitive adhesive tape).

**Also called:** double-sided foam tape; double-coated foam tape; acrylic foam tape; foam mounting tape; mounting tape; dimensional tape (thick double-sided tape; Wikipedia 'Double-sided tape'); moulding tape / molding tape (car badges and trim); emblem tape, attachment tape (car trade); structural bonding tape; PSA foam tape (pressure-sensitive adhesive); foam pads, sticky pads, mounting squares; PE foam tape and PU foam tape (cheaper tapes with a polyethylene or polyurethane foam core); VHB tape, 'Very High Bond' (3M brand); ACXplus (tesa brand); Scotch mounting tape (3M brand).

## How it works

The glue on each face is a pressure-sensitive adhesive (PSA): a soft, permanently tacky, viscoelastic material that flows into the tiny bumps of a surface when you press it, then resists flowing when loaded, so it sticks by molecular attraction (van der Waals forces) without drying or hardening (Wikipedia 'Pressure-sensitive adhesive'). Firm pressure gives more contact and a stronger bond, and the grip keeps building for up to about 3 days as the adhesive keeps flowing into the surface ('wet-out') (3M VHB 5952 data sheet). The foam core squashes to fill slightly uneven or mismatched surfaces, spreads the load along the whole joint instead of at a few screw points, lets two materials that expand differently with heat slide a little, and damps vibration (3M VHB Tape Design Guide). Because it is soft and viscoelastic it is stronger against quick loads than slow steady ones, and it slowly [creeps](../glossary.md#creep) under a constant weight, so it needs enough area for the load.

## Parts

Single piece: a foam core (acrylic foam in the strongest 'VHB-type' tapes, or polyethylene or polyurethane foam in cheaper tapes) with a layer of pressure-sensitive adhesive on each face and a peel-off protective strip (release liner). Optional extras: a surface [primer](../glossary.md#primer) or adhesion promoter, and a small hand roller (J-roller) to press it down

## Everyday examples

- Car badges, emblems and trim strips, which car makers fix in the factory with thin double-sided foam 'moulding tape' (MIG Welding Forum thread on refitting badges; 3M VHB Tape Design Guide lists trim and extrusion attachment)
- The sticky pad under an action-camera mount on a helmet or car (a tape seller says GoPro mounts use 3M VHB 4991 tape, 2.3 mm thick) (StickyTapes.com)
- Signs bonded to their frames, and stiffeners stuck inside metal panels with no visible screws (3M VHB Tape Design Guide)
- Foam mounting squares used at home to stick hooks, small mirrors and cable clips to walls and furniture ⚑ [uncertain]

## When to use it

When you want to mount something light or medium-weight on a smooth, clean surface without drilling holes (tiles, glass, painted metal, smooth plastics, a 3D-printed bracket onto a desk or enclosure). When joining different materials, such as metal to plastic or glass, which expand differently with heat. When screws would show, crack thin material or concentrate the load at a few points. When you also want the joint to seal out water and dust, or to damp vibration and rattles (e.g. mounting a sensor, circuit board, or lens window). When the parts are slightly uneven and need something that fills small gaps

## When not to use it

Not on rough, dusty, crumbly or unsealed porous surfaces such as raw wood, plaster, brick or concrete (use screws and wall plugs, or an epoxy or construction adhesive after sealing). Not on polypropylene, polyethylene, PTFE or silicone unless you use an LSE tape or primer (or use mechanical fasteners). Not for heavy, overhead or safety-critical loads, where creep or a peeling edge could drop the part: add screws or brackets. Not if you want to take the part off cleanly later (use stretch-release adhesive strips, hook-and-loop fasteners, magnets or screws). Not above about 93 °C long-term for acrylic foam, or on PLA parts in hot places. Not where the part must be positioned exactly (use pins, a jig or screws), or where the joint is loaded in [peel](../glossary.md#peel-vs-shear)

## Details

- **What it does (jobs):**
  - glue or bond: sticks two parts together on contact with no drying time
  - join and hold: mounts hooks, clips, sensors, badges, signs and panels to a surface without drilling
  - seal it, or pass cables or fluid through: a continuous closed-cell foam strip also seals the joint against water, moisture and dust (3M VHB 5952 data sheet; tesa PE foam tapes)
- **Movement:** none: a fixed joint. The soft foam does let the parts shift slightly sideways (up to about 3 times the tape thickness, per 3M) so that heat growth does not break it, and a joint can slowly creep under a steady load
- **Directions it can move (degrees of freedom):** 0: the parts are bonded together (apart from the small give of the foam)
- **Special behaviour:**
  - none: it simply holds. Useful side effects rather than listed behaviours: the foam absorbs shock and vibration, fills small gaps between uneven parts, and allows different heat growth of the two materials (3M VHB Tape Design Guide)
- **How it holds:**
  - [material bond](../glossary.md#form-fit--force-fit--material-bond) (glue, solvent or melting): a pressure-sensitive adhesive that wets the surface and holds by molecular attraction without hardening; the foam core carries the load between the two adhesive faces
- **Input and output:** not applicable
- **Power:** passive (no power needed)
- **Load:** Light to medium. Strong in shear (sliding one part along the other) and in straight pull when the whole area is loaded evenly; weakest in peel and cleavage (a load that lifts one edge first). Example for one 1.1 mm acrylic foam tape after 72 h: overlap shear 550 kPa, straight pull (normal tensile) 480 kPa, but 90° peel only 39 N per cm of width (3M VHB 5952 data sheet). For steady (static) weight 3M advises about 57 cm² of tape per kg (4 in² per lb) to avoid creep, and about 85 kPa (12 psi) as a design value for dynamic loads (3M VHB 5952 data sheet)
- **Play and precision:** Not a locating joint: the part ends up wherever you placed it by hand, so use a jig or pencil marks for alignment. No play when bonded, but the foam gives slightly under load, creeps under steady weight, and allows sideways movement of up to about 3 times the tape thickness between parts that grow differently with heat (3M VHB 5952 data sheet)
- **Taking it apart:** semi-permanent (can be undone but may damage parts): it is sold as a permanent bond, but it can be cut apart through the foam with a wire, fishing line or fine saw, softened with a heat gun, and the leftover adhesive removed with solvents or a rubber eraser wheel (3M technical bulletin on disassembly). Paint, soft plastics or print surfaces can be damaged; for clean removal use stretch-release strips instead
- **How often it can be reused:** once: the tape is single-use; the parts can usually be cleaned and re-taped
- **Sealing:** watertight: a continuous, unbroken loop of closed-cell foam tape seals against water, moisture and dust, which is why it is used to bond and seal lenses and windows into housings (3M VHB 5952 data sheet; 3M VHB Tape Design Guide; tesa PE foam tapes). Gaps or joins in the loop leak
- **Adjustable:** no: it grabs on contact, so the part can't be slid into position once it touches; cut it off and re-tape to move it
- **Access needed:** one side only (blind): you stick the part onto the surface from the front; nothing has to reach behind the wall or panel
- **Materials:** Tape: acrylic foam with acrylic adhesive (the strongest and most heat- and weather-resistant type), or polyethylene (PE) or polyurethane (PU) foam with acrylic or rubber adhesive (cheaper, lighter duty) (tesa acrylic and PE foam tape pages; Wikimedia Commons photo of PU foam tape). Bonds well to high-surface-energy materials: metals (aluminium, steel, copper), glass, and plastics such as ABS and polycarbonate (42 dynes/cm), PVC (39), acrylic (38), polyester (43, the family PETG belongs to) and nylon (46), plus many paints and powder coats with 'modified acrylic' tapes (3M/JBC LSE selection guide; 3M VHB Tape Design Guide). Bonds poorly to low-surface-energy plastics such as polypropylene (29 dynes/cm), polyethylene (31) and PTFE (18) unless you use a special low-surface-energy (LSE) tape or a primer such as 3M Primer 94 (3M/JBC LSE guide; 3M VHB Tape Design Guide). Needs extra care: bare wood, particleboard and concrete must be sealed first, glass needs a silane treatment for best results, and soft vinyl and rubber with plasticisers that migrate can weaken the bond (3M VHB 5952 data sheet). PLA prints: [surface energy](../glossary.md#surface-energy) not found in these sources, but makers report printed PLA hooks holding well ⚑ [uncertain]. Silicone rubber and silicone-coated surfaces: poor ⚑ [uncertain]
- **Heat, cold, water and wear:** Heat: a typical acrylic foam tape (3M VHB 5952) is rated about 93 °C long-term and 149 °C for short periods, and its steady-load holding halves between 23 °C and 66 °C and again by 93 °C (3M VHB 5952 data sheet). PSAs generally lose shear holding when hot and lose tack when cold (Wikipedia 'Pressure-sensitive adhesive'). Cold: apply above about 10 °C (0 °C for low-temperature tapes); below about -40 °C the tape turns rigid and a sharp knock can fracture it (3M disassembly bulletin). Water, UV and solvents: acrylic foam tapes are made to resist UV, moisture, temperature cycling and many solvents and seal against water (3M VHB Tape Design Guide; 3M VHB 5952 data sheet); cheaper PE-foam tapes with rubber adhesive are less heat- and weather-resistant ⚑ [uncertain]. Creep: under a constant load the viscoelastic foam slowly flows, so steady loads need about 57 cm² of tape per kg (3M). The printed part may be the weak point: PLA softens above about 60 °C (Prusa). Shelf life of unused tape about 24 months (3M VHB 5952 data sheet)

## Making it

- **Ways to make or get it:**
  - off-the-shelf part: rolls of tape in many widths and thicknesses, and die-cut pads
  - 3D print (FDM): design printed parts with a flat pad for the tape
  - 3D print (resin): the same; bond to fully cured, clean parts ⚑ [uncertain]
  - laser cut: acrylic and sealed plywood parts
  - woodwork: only onto sealed or painted wood
  - sheet metal: panels, stiffeners and brackets (3M VHB Tape Design Guide shows panel-to-frame and stiffener-to-panel bonds)
- **Printing it in one piece (print in place):** no: the tape is applied after printing (you could print a recess to locate the tape, but the tape itself is a separate part)
- **Tools:** To fit: scissors or a knife to cut the tape, isopropyl alcohol and water on a lint-free cloth to clean, and a small roller (J-roller) or firm hand pressure; optionally a primer or adhesion promoter for plastics and paints. To remove: fishing line, wire or a fine saw to cut through the foam, a heat gun, a plastic scraper, and an adhesive remover (citrus cleaner, bug-and-tar remover or MEK) (3M disassembly bulletin)
- **Difficulty:** easy: cut, clean, stick and press. The only real skills are cleaning the surface, pressing hard, giving it enough area, and waiting a day or more before loading it fully

### 3D printing tips

Give the part a flat mounting pad and put that face on the print bed: a surface printed directly on the bed is 'perfectly flat and smooth' (Prusa knowledge base), which gives the tape the most contact. A textured bed sheet makes that face bumpy, so use a smooth sheet for the pad face or a thicker, more conformable tape ⚑ [uncertain]. Watch for warped corners and [elephant's foot](../glossary.md#elephants-foot), because the tape conforms only a little and 'will not flow to fill large gaps' (3M VHB 5952 data sheet); a thick tape (about 1.1 mm or more) is suggested for rigid parts. Size the pad for the load: about 57 cm² of tape per kg of steady load (3M), so a 200 g holder needs roughly 11-12 cm² (for example two 19 mm x 30 mm pieces). Round the pad and tape corners, because pointed corners start to lift first (Wikipedia 'Pressure-sensitive adhesive'). Shape the part so the load pulls along the wall (shear) rather than prising the top edge off (peel): for a hook, make the pad tall and put the hook low on it ⚑ [uncertain]. Material: ABS, ASA, PETG and nylon are high-surface-energy plastics that bond well (3M/JBC chart lists ABS 42, polyester 43, nylon 46 dynes/cm); polypropylene prints need an LSE tape or primer. PLA softens above about 60 °C (Prusa), below the tape's own long-term limit of about 93 °C, so in a hot car or near a heater the print may fail before the tape. Clean the pad with isopropyl alcohol and water, press firmly (3M suggests about 100 kPa, 15 psi), and wait: about 50% of full strength after 20 minutes, 90% after 24 hours and 100% after 72 hours at room temperature (3M). In a test of tesa tapes on printed nylon (PA-12), a PU-foam tape (tesa 4976) was strongest and every tape got stronger with longer dwell time (Forerunner 3D). If you print a pocket for the tape, keep it shallower than the tape so the tape stands proud and gets squeezed ⚑ [uncertain]

## Rules of thumb

- Steady (static) loads: about 57 cm² of tape per kg (4 in² per lb) of weight to avoid excessive creep; dynamic loads: design at about 85 kPa (12 psi) (3M VHB Tape 5952 technical data sheet)
- Press the tape to about 100 kPa (15 psi) with a roller or platen; when bonding two rigid parts, press again after assembly so the whole bond line sees that pressure (3M VHB 5952 data sheet; 3M VHB Tape Design Guide)
- Bond strength at room temperature: about 50% after 20 minutes, 90% after 24 hours, 100% after 72 hours; 1 hour at 66 °C (150 °F) speeds this up and can raise the final strength (3M VHB 5952 data sheet)
- Apply at 21-38 °C ideally and not below 10-15 °C for most acrylic foam tapes (special low-temperature tapes go down to 0 °C); once applied, cold holding is generally fine (3M VHB 5952 data sheet; 3M VHB Tape Design Guide)
- Use tape at least 1.1 mm (45 mil) thick to bond rigid parts of normal flatness; thinner tape can be used as the parts get more flexible (3M VHB 5952 data sheet)
- The tape tolerates sideways (shear) movement between the two parts of up to about 3 times its thickness, e.g. from different heat expansion (3M VHB 5952 data sheet; 3M VHB Tape Design Guide)
- Clean with isopropyl alcohol and water (3M gives 50:50 in the design guide and 70:30 in the 5952 data sheet); degrease oily parts first, abrade (about 180 grit) oxidised or dirty surfaces, seal porous wood and concrete (3M VHB 5952 data sheet; 3M VHB Tape Design Guide)
- Surfaces below roughly the mid-30s in surface energy (dynes/cm), such as polypropylene (29), polyethylene (31) and PTFE (18), are hard to bond: use an LSE tape or a primer (3M LSE product selection guide via JBC Technologies)
- Peel is the weak direction: one 1.1 mm acrylic foam tape gives 550 kPa in overlap shear but only 39 N/cm in 90° peel (3M VHB 5952 data sheet), so design joints to load the tape in shear
- Heat roughly halves steady-load holding at each step: the 5952 tape held 1,000 g on 3.23 cm² for 10,000 minutes at 23 °C, 500 g at 66 °C and 250 g at 93 °C; its long-term temperature limit is 93 °C and short-term 149 °C (3M VHB 5952 data sheet)
- Round the corners of tapes and labels: pointed corners start to detach first (Wikipedia 'Pressure-sensitive adhesive')

## Common failures

Peels off from one edge or corner because the load prises the part away instead of sliding it (design for shear, round the corners, use a taller pad). Slowly slides or sags (creep) under a steady weight because the tape area is too small (use about 57 cm² per kg). Never stuck properly because the surface was dusty, oily, textured, warped or low-surface-energy plastic, the tape was applied in the cold, or it was not pressed hard enough (clean with IPA/water, prime plastics, press to about 100 kPa, apply above 10 °C). Loaded too early, before the bond built up over 24-72 hours. Pulls off a layer of paint, the top layers of a print, or the surface of unsealed wood instead of failing in the tape ⚑ [uncertain]. Loosens over time on soft vinyl or rubber whose plasticisers migrate into the adhesive (3M VHB 5952 data sheet). Lets go in a hot car when the tape or the PLA part softens

## Safety

Low hazard in itself. Isopropyl alcohol, primers and adhesive removers are flammable and give off fumes (3M lists a flash point of -20 °C for Primer 94); use them in a ventilated place away from flames (3M VHB Tape Design Guide; 3M disassembly bulletin). Take care with heat guns, knives and wires when removing tape. Don't rely on tape alone where a falling object could hurt someone (heavy items, overhead, children's rooms): creep, heat, or a peeling edge can let go without warning ⚑ [uncertain]

## Related

- Stretch-release adhesive strips: also stick without drilling, but pull off cleanly by stretching; weaker and meant for light, temporary mounting
- Contact adhesive (contact cement): a liquid that also bonds on contact, but forms a thin, stiff glue line with no foam to fill gaps or damp vibration
- Two-part epoxy and structural acrylic adhesives: much stronger and stiffer, and fill gaps, but need mixing, clamping and [cure](../glossary.md#cure) time and are hard to undo
- Hot-melt adhesive (glue gun): quick and gap-filling, but weak on smooth surfaces and softens in the heat
- Hook-and-loop fastener: usually attached with its own adhesive backing; lets you remove and refit the part
- Magnet joint (embedded magnets, magnetic catch): removable mounting on steel surfaces
- Silicone and MS-polymer adhesive sealants: flexible, gap-filling bond and seal that needs curing time
- Glued joint design (butt, lap and scarf joints; peel vs shear): how to shape any bonded joint so it is loaded in shear rather than peel
- Gel 'nano tape': a clear acrylic gel tape without a foam core, used the same way for light items
