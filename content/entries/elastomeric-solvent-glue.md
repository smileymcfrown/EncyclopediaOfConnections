An elastomeric solvent glue is a synthetic rubber dissolved in a strong solvent; when the solvent evaporates the rubber is left behind as a clear, tough, stretchy layer that grips most materials and keeps the joint flexible and waterproof.

**In plain words:** A thick, clear, gooey glue from a tube that smells strongly of solvent and dries into a tough rubbery layer, so the joint can still bend and stretch; used to stick fabric, shoes, rhinestones, rubber, glass, metal and wood to each other. **Proper term:** Elastomeric solvent-based adhesive (rubber-based solvent glue; flexible multi-purpose craft adhesive).

**Also called:** flexible craft glue, industrial-strength craft adhesive, multi-purpose adhesive, jewellery glue, shoe glue / shoe repair adhesive (manufacturer data sheets); rubber-based adhesive, solvent-based adhesive, drying adhesive (Wikipedia 'Adhesive': solvent-based adhesives harden as the solvent evaporates); styrene-butadiene (SBR) adhesive, from the synthetic rubber it is made of (Goop and Shoe Goo safety data sheets; Wikipedia 'Shoe Goo'); E6000, E6000 Craft, E6000 Jewelry & Bead, E6000 Premium, E6000 Fabri-Fuse (brands, Eclectic Products); Amazing Goop, Household Goop, Shoe Goo (brands, Eclectic Products); B-7000, T-7000, E-7000 (generic clear 'phone and rhinestone' glues sold online; described as solvent-based and semi-flexible, but their exact recipe varies and is not published) ⚑ [uncertain]; Loctite Vinyl, Fabric & Plastic (now 'Vinyl, Fabric & Leather') Flexible Adhesive (brand): a thinner, polyurethane-in-solvent cousin (Henkel data sheet).

## How it works

The tube holds a rubbery plastic (a styrene-butadiene copolymer in Goop and Shoe Goo) dissolved in solvents such as toluene and petroleum naphtha; about 45-49% of the weight is solids (the rubber and resins) and the rest evaporates (Goop and Shoe Goo safety data sheets; E6000 Craft, Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets). When you spread it on the parts, the solvent wets the surfaces and starts to evaporate; after about 2 minutes it becomes tacky, it is 'set' in 20-45 minutes and fully cured in 24 hours for a thin film or 48-72 hours for a thick one (E6000 Craft, Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets). Nothing chemically reacts: it simply dries (a drying adhesive, Wikipedia 'Adhesive'), and what is left is a soft rubber (80 Shore A hardness) that can stretch by 800-1,100% before breaking, so the bond bends with the parts instead of cracking (E6000 Craft and Industrial data sheets). You can use it 'wet' (press the parts together straight away and hold or clamp them) or like a contact adhesive (coat both faces, wait about 2 minutes, then press: it grabs immediately and is hard to reposition) (E6000 Craft data sheet).

## Parts

Single piece: one thick liquid or gel in a tube, no mixing. Parts: the glue, the tube with a piercing cap or precision nozzle; optional helpers are sandpaper, a cleaning solvent (acetone or citrus cleaner for wet glue), clamps, tape or clothes pegs, and a syringe or fine nozzle for small jobs (E6000 Craft data sheet; Printables syringe adaptor).

## Everyday examples

- Re-attaching a flapping sole or rebuilding a worn heel with Shoe Goo (Shoe Goo data sheet; Wikipedia 'Shoe Goo')
- Rhinestones and gems glued onto clothing, bags and jewellery findings (E6000 Premium Jewelry & Bead data sheet)
- Patching snowboard boots, waders and rubber boots (Wikimedia Commons photo; Shoe Goo data sheet)
- Household fixes such as a loose towel rail or a broken teacup (Amazing Goop data sheet)

## When to use it

Use it when the joint must bend, stretch or take knocks and vibration, or when you are joining two different materials: fabric, leather, rubber, vinyl and TPU prints to themselves or to wood, metal, glass or rigid plastic (E6000 Craft and Industrial data sheets). It is good for jewellery and craft work (clear, gap-filling, washer and dryer safe), for shoe and outdoor-gear repairs where it also waterproofs and seals, and for small parts that a [brittle](../glossary.md#brittle--ductile) glue would pop off when flexed (Shoe Goo and Amazing Goop data sheets; Snapmaker).

## When not to use it

Avoid it for rigid, load-bearing plastic or metal joints: use two-part epoxy, CA or a structural adhesive (Snapmaker). Avoid it on PE, PP, polystyrene foam and paper (E6000 Craft data sheet): use a low-surface-energy adhesive or mechanical fixing. Don't use it where you need handling strength quickly: CA sets in seconds. Don't use it for food, drinking-water or pet items (E6000 Craft data sheet), for parts exposed to fuel or strong solvents that dissolve it (petrol, toluene), in constant sunlight without paint or a UV grade (E6000 Industrial and Goop data sheets), or near heat above about 66-82 °C (data sheets). If the fumes are a problem, choose a solvent-free or water-based flexible adhesive such as an MS-polymer or silicone adhesive sealant (e.g. E6000 Plus is solvent-free; E6000 Plus data sheet). For large flat areas (laminates, foam sheets) a brush-on contact cement is usually easier (thisTOthat; Wikipedia 'Adhesive').

## Details

- **What it does (jobs):**
  - glue or bond: permanent, flexible bonds between the same or different materials, e.g. rhinestones to fabric, rubber to metal, glass to wood (E6000 Craft and Industrial data sheets)
  - join and hold: holds small hardware, trims, beads and findings in place; one manufacturer also lists 'locking down fasteners against vibration' (E6000 Industrial data sheet)
  - seal it, or pass cables or fluid through: seals and waterproofs boots, waders and fabrics and fills small cracks; it is sold as an 'adhesive & sealant' (Shoe Goo and Amazing Goop data sheets)
- **Movement:** flex: the cured glue is rubbery, so the joint bends and stretches with flexible materials such as fabric, leather, vinyl and rubber, and absorbs vibration (E6000 Industrial data sheet). It does not let parts move in a controlled way.
- **Directions it can move (degrees of freedom):** 0: the parts are bonded together; the rubbery glue line only gives a little elastic movement
- **Special behaviour:**
  - none: it has none of the listed behaviours. Its own tricks are staying flexible even in cold weather (does not become brittle), filling small gaps with a thick bead that can be built up (e.g. rebuilding a worn shoe sole), and doubling as a waterproof sealant or coating (E6000, Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets)
- **How it holds:**
  - [material bond](../glossary.md#form-fit--force-fit--material-bond) (glue, solvent or melting): the dried rubber sticks to both surfaces; on some plastics the solvent also softens the surface slightly so the glue 'bites' in (E6000 Premium data sheet says it bonds most 3D-printing plastics, ABS, polycarbonate, PVC and acrylic) ⚑ [uncertain: the bite mechanism is inferred, not stated by the manufacturer]
- **Input and output:** not applicable
- **Power:** passive (no power needed): it dries by solvent evaporation at room temperature; the ideal working temperature is 10-32 °C (E6000 Craft data sheet).
- **Load:** light to medium ⚑ [uncertain]: manufacturers publish the strength of the cured rubber film itself (tensile strength about 2,000-3,800 psi, roughly 14-26 MPa, with 800-1,100% stretch; E6000, Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets), not the strength of a glued joint. A related flexible solvent glue (Loctite Vinyl, Fabric & Leather, polyurethane-based) gives about 12-17 lb per inch of width in a T-peel test on vinyl, polycarbonate and SBR rubber (Henkel data sheet). In one maker's hanging-bucket test on 6 x 6 mm glued PLA pads, that Loctite glue failed at about 5.9-9.6 kg (roughly 1.6-2.6 MPa), roughly half to three-quarters of the load reached by a rigid 'hard plastic' glue (12.9-14.8 kg), but it stretched and yielded before failing rather than snapping (Printables 1352973). Good in [peel](../glossary.md#peel-vs-shear) and impact on flexible parts because it stretches; weaker and creepier than rigid glues under a steady structural load ⚑ [uncertain].
- **Play and precision:** not applicable (it does not locate parts). The thick glue fills small gaps and uneven surfaces, but the rubbery layer lets parts shift slightly under load, so it is not for precise alignment; use a jig, tape or locating pins.
- **Taking it apart:** semi-permanent (can be undone but may damage parts): wet glue wipes off with acetone or citrus cleaner, but cured glue has to be cut or scraped away (E6000 Craft data sheet); it is dissolved by strong solvents such as toluene, petrol (gasoline), cyclohexane and perchloroethylene (E6000 Craft data sheet), which may also attack the parts.
- **How often it can be reused:** once: the glue is single-use; to redo a joint, cut or scrape the old rubber off and re-glue
- **Sealing:** watertight: after full [cure](../glossary.md#cure) it is waterproof and can be submerged in fresh and salt water, and it is used to seal boots, waders and waterproof fabrics and to fill small cracks (E6000 Industrial, Amazing Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets). It is not a pressure seal.
- **Adjustable:** Only while wet: parts can be slid into position for a few minutes (the Loctite flexible adhesive 'can be repositioned while the adhesive is still wet'), but once two tacky coated faces are pressed together the bond is immediate and 'difficult to reposition' (E6000 Craft data sheet; Henkel data sheet). After curing, no.
- **Access needed:** not applicable: glue goes on the joint faces before assembly, or is run along a seam or crack from one side
- **Materials:** Goop and Shoe Goo are a styrene-butadiene synthetic rubber in toluene and petroleum naphtha (safety data sheets); current US E6000 Craft also uses toluene and petroleum distillates, but the sheets opened here don't name its rubber ⚑ [uncertain]. Older and Canadian formulas used perchloroethylene, and E6000 Premium uses n-propyl acetate (2013 E6000 SDS; 2016 and 2025 E6000 Craft data sheets; thisTOthat; E6000 Premium data sheet). Note the conflict: thisTOthat calls Household Goop 'silicone-based', but the manufacturer's safety data sheet lists styrene-butadiene rubber and no silicone. Bonds well: wood, metal, glass, fibreglass, ceramics, masonry and concrete, leather, rubber, vinyl, PVC, neoprene, fabric and many plastics (E6000 Craft and Industrial data sheets); gems, beads, polymer clay and jewellery metals (E6000 Premium Jewelry & Bead). E6000 Premium claims to bond most 3D-printing plastics, ABS, polycarbonate, acrylic and polystyrene. Makers report good results on PLA (Blue Paper Tech) and on flexible TPU prints (Snapmaker), but no manufacturer test on PLA, PETG or TPU was found ⚑ [uncertain]. Poor or not recommended: polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP), polystyrene and Styrofoam (for standard E6000), paper, and finished surfaces that the solvent may damage (E6000 Craft and Industrial data sheets). Not for anything in contact with food, drinking water or animals (E6000 Craft data sheet).
- **Heat, cold, water and wear:** Heat: E6000 and Amazing Goop withstand -40 to 82 °C (-40 to 180 °F) intermittently, Shoe Goo -40 to 66 °C (E6000 Craft, Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets); the Loctite flexible polyurethane type is 'not for applications over 140 °F (60 °C)' (Henkel data sheet). Cold: stays flexible and 'does not become brittle in cold weather' (E6000 Craft data sheet). Water: waterproof after full cure, can be submerged in fresh and salt water, and survives washing machines and tumble dryers but not dry cleaning (E6000 Industrial and Amazing Goop data sheets). Sunlight: no UV resistance; paint over it or use a UV grade (Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets; E6000 Industrial). Chemicals: excellent resistance to water, dilute acids and bases, antifreeze and motor oil; thin films immersed for two weeks gained under 2% weight and lost under 10% strength, but it is dissolved by petrol, toluene, cyclohexane, perchloroethylene and propyl acetate (Shoe Goo and E6000 Craft data sheets). [Creep](../glossary.md#creep): as a soft rubber it can slowly give under a constant load ⚑ [uncertain].

## Making it

- **Ways to make or get it:**
  - off-the-shelf part: tubes (from 5 mL minis to cartridges), precision-tip and 'jewellery' versions, and clear, black or white colours (E6000 data sheets)
  - 3D print (FDM): gluing prints to fabric, leather or other materials, and bonding flexible TPU parts, where a rigid glue would crack (Snapmaker; Prusa notes that contact cements for shoe repairs 'might be good especially for flexible filament materials')
  - sewing, cosplay and textiles: fabric, trims, rhinestones and patches; it is washer and dryer safe but should not be dry-cleaned (E6000 Craft and Goop data sheets)
  - jewellery and beading: stones, crystals and beads on findings; securing knots (E6000 Premium Jewelry & Bead data sheet)
  - shoe and outdoor-gear repair: re-attaching soles, rebuilding worn heels, sealing boots and waders (Shoe Goo data sheet)
  - woodwork and household repair: small repairs where materials differ or the joint must flex (Amazing Goop data sheet)
- **Printing it in one piece (print in place):** no: it is a glue for joining separately made parts, although you can print shallow recesses or textured pads to take a bead of glue
- **Tools:** The glue (pierce the foil seal with the cap), sandpaper to lightly roughen smooth surfaces, a clean cloth, and something to hold the parts while it sets (tape, clothes pegs, clamps, rubber bands). For fine work a toothpick, syringe with a blunt needle or a precision nozzle helps (E6000 Craft data sheet; Printables syringe adaptor). Acetone or citrus cleaner for wet squeeze-out. Work with good ventilation.
- **Difficulty:** easy: squeeze, press and wait. The main difficulties are the long cure (a full day or more), keeping squeeze-out off visible surfaces, and working with strong solvent fumes

### 3D printing tips

1) Use it for the jobs where flexibility matters: printed parts onto fabric, straps, leather or rubber, and TPU to TPU or TPU to rigid parts. For rigid PLA-to-PLA assemblies it is 'safe, but it cures flexibly', so CA or epoxy give stiffer joints (Snapmaker). 2) Lightly roughen the faces and make sure they are clean and dry (E6000 Craft data sheet); a slightly rough print surface is fine, since one maker chose it because it 'glues slightly rough surfaces', 'sets up in about an hour' and doesn't discolour PLA like superglue can (Blue Paper Tech, 2017). 3) Give the solvent a way out: it cures by evaporation, and 'the thicker the layer of adhesive, the longer it will take to dry' (thisTOthat), so use thin beads and allow 24 hours (thin) to 48-72 hours (thick) before loading (E6000 Craft data sheet). Large, tight, non-porous overlaps may stay soft inside for longer ⚑ [uncertain]. 4) For a quick grab, coat both faces, wait about 2 minutes until tacky, then press; for adjustable positioning, join while wet and tape or clamp (E6000 Craft data sheet). 5) The solvents attack some plastics: E6000 is not recommended on polystyrene (so test on HIPS), and makers report that E6000 softens or glues printed PLA and PETG nozzles and made an ABS tube cap brittle and cracked; print tools that touch the glue in TPU or treat them as disposable (E6000 Craft data sheet; Printables 1101848 and 1133417) ⚑ [uncertain]. 6) Print a small applicator: a Luer-lock syringe adaptor or fine nozzle lets you place tiny amounts on rhinestones or thin prints (Printables 3660 and 1101848). 7) Check outdoors use: standard E6000 and Goop have no UV resistance, so paint over the joint or choose a UV-resistant grade (E6000 Industrial and Goop data sheets). 8) Test the bond on scrap prints first: glue two printed test pieces and pull or hang weights from them (Printables 1352973).

### Buying one (off-the-shelf parts)

Search for 'flexible multi-purpose adhesive', 'industrial strength craft adhesive', 'rubber-based adhesive', 'SBR adhesive', 'shoe repair adhesive', 'jewelry and bead adhesive', 'fabric adhesive (solvent-based)' and 'adhesive sealant'. Common brands: E6000 Craft / Industrial / Premium / Jewelry & Bead / Fabri-Fuse, Amazing Goop, Shoe Goo (Eclectic Products); Loctite Vinyl, Fabric & Leather Flexible Adhesive (Henkel). ## Rules of thumb

- Tack time about 2 minutes, set time 20-45 minutes, full cure 24 hours for a thin film and 48-72 hours for a thick one; maximum strength may take up to 72 hours (E6000 Craft, Amazing Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets).
- Apply between 10 and 32 °C (50-90 °F); cured E6000 and Goop take -40 to 82 °C intermittently, Shoe Goo -40 to 66 °C (E6000 Craft, Amazing Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets).
- The cured rubber is about 80 Shore A with 800-1,100% elongation and 2,000-3,800 psi tensile strength (ASTM D-412 film tests; E6000 Craft, Amazing Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets). These are film properties, not joint strength.
- About 45-49% of the tube's weight (42-46% of its volume) is solids; the rest (toluene and petroleum distillates, about 450-490 g/L VOC) evaporates into the room (E6000 Craft, Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets).
- Roughen smooth surfaces and keep them clean and dry; do not use on PE, PP, polystyrene, Styrofoam or paper (E6000 Craft and Industrial data sheets).
- Not UV resistant: paint over it for outdoor use in sunlight (Amazing Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets: 'UV resistance: None; paint-over for UV resistance').
- Unopened shelf life 2 years; if it thickens in the cold, warm it to room temperature; a thin coat of petroleum jelly on the tube threads stops the cap sticking (E6000 Craft data sheet).

## Common failures

Loading the joint too early: it only sets in 20-45 minutes and needs 24-72 hours to reach full strength, longer for thick beads (data sheets; thisTOthat). Parts that simply peel off PE, PP or polystyrene, or off greasy, dusty or glossy surfaces; roughen and clean first (E6000 Craft data sheet). Moving the parts after two tacky faces have touched: the bond grabs at once and repositioning weakens it (E6000 Craft data sheet). Joints that sag or creep because the glue is soft rubber, when a rigid glue was needed (Snapmaker) ⚑ [uncertain]. Sunlight breaking down unpainted glue outdoors (Goop and Shoe Goo data sheets). Solvent damage: crazed or melted polystyrene, foam or painted finishes, and printed applicators that go soft or brittle (E6000 Craft data sheet; Printables 1101848 and 1133417). Glue smeared on visible faces; wipe wet glue with acetone or citrus cleaner, cut away cured glue (E6000 Craft data sheet). A cap glued shut; smear petroleum jelly on the tube threads (E6000 Craft data sheet).

## Safety

The solvents are the main hazard. US Goop and Shoe Goo contain 25-50% toluene plus petroleum naphtha: highly flammable liquid and vapour (flash point about 0-4 °C for Goop), harmful if swallowed, eye and skin irritant, may cause drowsiness or dizziness, suspected of damaging the unborn child, and may damage organs with prolonged or repeated exposure (Goop and Shoe Goo safety data sheets; E6000 Craft data sheet California Prop 65 warning). Older and Canadian formulas used perchloroethylene (tetrachloroethylene), a suspected carcinogen (2013 E6000 SDS; thisTOthat), so read the label of the tube you have. Work with good ventilation, away from flames and sparks, keep it away from children, and never deliberately inhale it (Henkel label: misuse by inhaling 'may be harmful or fatal'). Wear gloves (nitrile recommended for the Loctite version; Henkel data sheet). It can damage painted or finished surfaces and some plastics before it dries (E6000 Craft data sheet). Don't use it on items for food, drinking water or animals (E6000 Craft data sheet).

## Related

- Contact adhesive (contact cement): also a rubber in solvent (often polychloroprene), but brushed thin on both faces and left to dry before pressing, for large flat areas such as laminates and shoe soles; no repositioning (Wikipedia 'Adhesive'; thisTOthat)
- Rubber cement: a weaker natural-rubber solvent glue for paper and crafts that rubs off cleanly (Wikipedia 'Rubber cement')
- Silicone and MS-polymer adhesive sealants: also flexible and waterproof, but cure with air moisture rather than by evaporation, with less or no solvent (e.g. solvent-free E6000 Plus)
- Cyanoacrylate (superglue), including rubber-toughened CA: sets in seconds and is much stiffer; better for rigid small parts, worse for fabric and flexing joints
- Two-part epoxy: rigid, gap-filling and strong for structural joints; cracks if the joint flexes
- Hot-melt adhesive (glue gun): instant, also slightly flexible, but weak and softens with heat
- Solvent welding (solvent cement): fuses plastics such as ABS, PVC or acrylic into one piece; rigid
- Polyurethane flexible solvent adhesives, e.g. vinyl and inflatable repair glues: thinner liquids for vinyl, PVC and fabric patches (Henkel data sheet)
- Sewing, rivets and snap fasteners: mechanical alternatives for fabric and leather that don't need solvent
