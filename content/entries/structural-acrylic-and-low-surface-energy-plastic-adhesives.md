LSE adhesives are special structural acrylic glues, or superglue used after a polyolefin [primer](../glossary.md#primer), that can make strong bonds to polyethylene, polypropylene and similar slippery plastics that normal glues can't wet or grip.

**In plain words:** Special glues for the slippery, waxy-feeling plastics that ordinary glue just [peels](../glossary.md#peel-vs-shear) off, such as polyethylene (milk jugs, chopping boards) and polypropylene (bottle caps, storage boxes): either a two-part glue from a double cartridge, or a kit where you paint on a primer and then use superglue. **Proper term:** Low-surface-energy (LSE) plastic adhesives: structural acrylic (methacrylate) polyolefin bonders, and polyolefin primers used with cyanoacrylate.

**Also called:** LSE adhesive, polyolefin bonder, PE/PP glue, 'glue for hard-to-bond plastics' (3M white paper; Loctite design guide); structural acrylic, toughened acrylic, reactive acrylic, methacrylate or MMA (methyl methacrylate) adhesive (Permabond; Loctite design guide); no-mix acrylic (resin on one part, activator on the other) and static-mix acrylic (two parts mixed in a nozzle) (Loctite design guide); polyolefin primer, CA primer or surface primer; kits sold as a 'plastics bonding system' with an activator pen (Loctite SF 770 data sheet; Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet); 3M Scotch-Weld DP8005 / DP8010, Loctite 3032, Permabond TA4610 (brands of LSE structural acrylics); Loctite 770 / SF 770 primer and Loctite Plastics Bonding System (brands of CA-plus-primer); 'plastic welder' glues, e.g. Devcon Plastic Welder (brand): general-purpose methacrylates for ABS, PVC and fibreglass that are not made for PE or PP (Devcon data sheet).

## How it works

Glue can only stick if it spreads out and wets the surface, but polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP) and PTFE have very low [surface energy](../glossary.md#surface-energy) (about 31 mN/m for PE and PP, 18.5 for PTFE, compared with about 1,000 for glass), so most liquid glues bead up on them 'like raindrops on a freshly waxed car' and make weak bonds (3M white paper). LSE structural acrylics are two-part methacrylate glues: rubber dissolved in acrylic monomers, which a peroxide catalyst sets off into a tough, rubber-toughened plastic, and which are specially formulated to grip polyolefins without any surface treatment (Loctite design guide, p. 12; 3M DP8005 data sheet). The primer route paints on an amine dissolved in a fast-evaporating solvent; it acts as a chemical bridge that sticks to the plastic and triggers superglue, so the superglue can then bond (Loctite design guide, pp. 6, 75; Loctite SF 770 data sheet). A third route raises the plastic's surface energy with a brief flame, corona or plasma treatment so that other glues can wet it (Loctite design guide, p. 75; 3M white paper).

## Parts

A few parts. Route 1, structural acrylic: a double cartridge of resin and activator, a static mixing nozzle and an applicator gun (3M DP8005 data sheet; Permabond TA4610 data sheet). Route 2, primer plus superglue: a felt-tip or brush-on primer and a bottle of cyanoacrylate (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet). Optional: a propane torch with a flame spreader for flame treatment, used with some other glues (TAP Poly-Weld data sheet).

## Everyday examples

- The two-tube 'plastics bonding system' kit (superglue plus a felt-tip primer pen) sold for fixing polypropylene and polyethylene toys, figurines and household items (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet)
- Car bumper fascia, trim and plastic fuel or liquid containers bonded with LSE structural acrylics in factories (3M white paper)
- Things made of the plastics these glues are for: milk and household-chemical bottles, squeeze bottles, screw caps and toys (Loctite design guide, p. 50; Wikipedia 'Polyolefin')

## When to use it

Use an LSE structural acrylic when you need a strong, tough joint on polyethylene or polypropylene, or between PE/PP and metal, wood or another plastic, and screws, clips or welding won't do; for example gluing HDPE sheet, repairing a PP box or bonding a PP print to an aluminium bracket (3M DP8005 data sheet; Permabond TA4610 data sheet). Use the primer-plus-superglue kit for small, close-fitting indoor repairs to PP, PE, PTFE or rubber parts where a quick, no-mixing fix is enough (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet). General structural acrylics (MMA) are also good whenever you need a fast, tough, peel-resistant bond on ABS, PVC, polycarbonate, fibreglass or metal (Permabond MMA article; Devcon data sheet).

## When not to use it

Don't use ordinary superglue, epoxy, polyurethane glue, general-purpose MMA or silicone on PE or PP and expect it to hold: they give almost no strength there (Loctite design guide, pp. 51, 63). Avoid the primer-plus-superglue kit outdoors, for [gap filling](../glossary.md#gap-filling), for flexible joints, for parts that hold hot liquids or go in an oven or microwave, and wherever the joint will be peeled (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet; Loctite SF 770 data sheet). Avoid LSE acrylics on PTFE, silicone, nylon or oily and anti-static plastics without testing (3M DP8005 data sheet), and where the joint gets hot, since DP8005 keeps only about a quarter of its strength at 82 °C (3M DP8005 data sheet). Often a better answer for PE and PP is plastic welding (hot air, a speed-tip welder or a soldering iron), which 'readily' gives high-strength joints (Wikipedia 'Polyethylene'; Wikipedia 'Plastic welding'; see the Melted plastic weld entry), or mechanical fixing: screws, clips, snap fits or rivets (3M white paper). Solvent welding does not work, because polyolefins resist solvents (Wikipedia 'Polyolefin').

## Details

- **What it does (jobs):**
  - glue or bond: joins PE, PP and other low-surface-energy plastics to themselves or to metal, wood and other plastics (3M DP8005 data sheet; Permabond TA4610 data sheet)
- **Movement:** none: a rigid joint with no movement
- **Directions it can move (degrees of freedom):** 0: the glued parts are locked together
- **Special behaviour:**
  - none: it has none of the listed behaviours. Its trick is sticking to plastics that almost nothing else sticks to; the acrylics are also rubber-toughened, so they resist peel and impact better than most rigid glues (Permabond MMA article; Loctite design guide)
- **How it holds:**
  - [material bond](../glossary.md#form-fit--force-fit--material-bond) (glue, solvent or melting): the cured acrylic or primed superglue sticks to both surfaces
- **Input and output:** not applicable
- **Power:** passive (no power needed): the two-part acrylics [cure](../glossary.md#cure) at room temperature once mixed, and primed superglue cures from surface moisture; heating the joint to 66-80 °C for 30 minutes only speeds up the acrylic's cure (3M DP8005 data sheet).
- **Load:** medium to heavy for plastics. The right LSE glue is usually stronger than the PE or PP itself: 3M DP8005 gave about 1,100 psi (7.6 MPa) overlap shear on HDPE and PP, with the plastic breaking first (3M DP8005 data sheet); Permabond TA4610 gave more than 8 MPa on PP (the plastic broke) and 15 MPa on HDPE (Permabond TA4610 data sheet); primed superglue gave about 15 MPa on PP (Loctite SF 770 data sheet). 3M notes that by convention a glue counts as structural above 1,000 psi (about 7 MPa) overlap shear (3M white paper). Without the right product, bonds on PE and PP are almost nil: general-purpose superglue, epoxies, polyurethane, general-purpose methacrylate and silicone all gave only about 0.3-1.4 MPa or less (Loctite design guide, pp. 51, 63). Peel: the acrylics are toughened (DP8005 T-peel of 17 pounds per inch on HDPE with the plastic failing), but primed superglue is 'not recommended in assemblies where high peel strength is required' (3M DP8005 data sheet; Loctite SF 770 data sheet).
- **Play and precision:** not applicable (nothing moves). The glue layer has to be the right thickness: 3M wants a 0.005-0.008 in (0.13-0.2 mm) bondline and puts 0.008 in glass microspheres in the glue to set it, warning that a layer thinner than 0.005 in gives unpredictable results (3M DP8005 data sheet). Permabond TA4610 fills gaps up to 1 mm (Permabond TA4610 data sheet). The primer-plus-superglue kit needs close-fitting parts and is 'not suitable for... gap-filling' (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet).
- **Taking it apart:** permanent: on PE and PP the plastic itself usually breaks before the glue lets go (3M DP8005 data sheet: 'SF' substrate failure; Loctite design guide, p. 63). Strong solvents such as acetone and toluene weaken the cured acrylic (3M DP8005 chemical exposure table), but that is not a practical way to take a joint apart.
- **How often it can be reused:** once
- **Sealing:** none: it is a glue, not a sealant, although the structural acrylics resist water well and one is sold as able to bond underwater (3M DP8005 data sheet; Permabond TA4610 data sheet).
- **Adjustable:** no, and there is little time to line parts up: 3M DP8005 has a work life of only 2.5-3 minutes at 23 °C (3M DP8005 data sheet), Permabond TA4610 a [pot life](../glossary.md#pot-life--working-time) of 5-8 minutes (Permabond TA4610 data sheet), and primed superglue sets in about 30 seconds, with the instructions saying 'do not reposition parts' (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet).
- **Access needed:** not applicable: the glue goes on the joint faces before assembly
- **Materials:** Bonds well: polypropylene (PP), polyethylene (HDPE, LDPE, UHMWPE), thermoplastic polyolefin (TPO), and with the acrylics also ABS, PVC, polycarbonate, acrylic (PMMA), polystyrene, fibreglass, PET and PETG, glass, wood, concrete, aluminium and steel, so it is good for joining PE or PP to something else (3M DP8005 data sheet; Permabond TA4610 data sheet). Primed superglue also bonds PTFE and thermoplastic rubber (Loctite SF 770 data sheet). Poor or not recommended: 3M DP8005 lists PTFE, silicone, nylon, polyimide and surfaces with mould-release agents, oils or anti-static additives as not recommended (3M DP8005 data sheet); PTFE bonds are weak even with Permabond TA4610 (more than 2 MPa) (Permabond TA4610 data sheet). The primer-plus-superglue kit does not suit polystyrene foam, foam rubber, glass, glazed surfaces or silicone rubber (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet). Ordinary methacrylate 'plastic welder' glues don't list PE or PP at all (Devcon data sheet; Loctite design guide: general-purpose H3000 gave under 0.3 MPa on PP).
- **Heat, cold, water and wear:** Heat: 3M DP8005's overlap shear on HDPE falls from about 1,100 psi at 23 °C to about 700 psi at 49 °C, 500 psi at 66 °C and 300 psi at 82 °C (Tg onset about 33 °C); it keeps about 750-875 psi at -29 °C (3M DP8005 data sheet). Permabond TA4610 can be used down to -55 °C (Permabond TA4610 data sheet). Primed superglue is rated to 82 °C (180 °F) in the consumer kit (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet). Water: the acrylics resist water and humidity well; DP8005 kept 950-1,000 psi on HDPE after 14 days in 71 °C water, and TA4610 is sold as bonding underwater (3M DP8005 data sheet; Permabond TA4610 data sheet). The consumer primer kit is 'not suitable for exterior applications' (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet), although industrial primed superglue kept full strength after 1,000 hours in water or 95% humidity at 40 °C (Loctite SF 770 data sheet). Chemicals: gasoline, toluene and acetone badly weaken DP8005 (down to about 25-450 psi); diesel, antifreeze, salt water, isopropyl alcohol and dilute acid or alkali had little effect (3M DP8005 data sheet). Sunlight: cured DP8005 yellows with time (3M DP8005 data sheet). Storage: keep DP8005 cartridges at 4 °C or below for best shelf life, and primer at 8-21 °C (3M DP8005 data sheet; Loctite SF 770 data sheet).

## Making it

- **Ways to make or get it:**
  - off-the-shelf part: LSE structural acrylic cartridges, polyolefin primers and primer-plus-superglue kits
  - 3D print (FDM): joining PP or PE prints, or PETG, ABS, ASA and PC prints to each other or to metal ⚑ [uncertain]: no maker test of these glues on printed PP was found
  - CNC and sheet work: bonding HDPE or PP sheet (e.g. chopping-board plastic) and plastic lumber (3M white paper)
  - repairs: plastic toys, containers, bumpers and trim, wiper blades and rubber seals (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet; 3M white paper)
- **Printing it in one piece (print in place):** no: it is a glue for joining separate parts.
- **Tools:** For structural acrylics: the double cartridge, the matching applicator gun and mixing nozzles (hand mixing is not recommended for 10:1 products), isopropyl alcohol wipes, fine sandpaper (150 grit or finer), clamps or tape giving light pressure, gloves and good ventilation (3M DP8005 data sheet; Permabond TA4610 data sheet). For the primer route: the primer pen or bottle, superglue and tissue (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet). For flame treatment: a propane torch with a flame spreader and a water spray to check the result (TAP Poly-Weld data sheet).
- **Difficulty:** moderate: the primer-plus-superglue kit is easy and cheap, but the structural acrylics need a special cartridge and gun, have only a few minutes' working time, smell strongly and are not cheap; flame treatment needs care with a torch (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet; 3M DP8005 data sheet; Permabond MMA article; Hackaday comments).

### 3D printing tips

1) Know what you are gluing: PP is so hard to stick to that PP prints only grip a PP bed sheet or PP tape (Prusa knowledge base), which is the same problem glue has. For PLA, PETG, ABS and ASA prints, ordinary superglue or epoxy usually works, though methacrylate glues were among the strongest on PETG in Cosel's test (Hackaday). 2) Clean with isopropyl alcohol and let it evaporate; lightly sand with 150-grit or finer paper; avoid anti-static or oily additives and mould-release sprays (3M DP8005 data sheet; Permabond TA4610 data sheet). 3) With structural acrylics, use the maker's gun and mixing nozzle, throw away the first bit of mixed glue, and close the joint within the 2.5-3 minute work life (3M DP8005 data sheet). Design the joint so the glue layer is about 0.13-0.2 mm thick, and add small bumps or ribs about that height if parts sit flat (3M DP8005 data sheet: 0.005-0.008 in bondline). Clamp lightly (about 4-8 psi) for at least 2 hours; 3M notes plastic parts can be designed to hold themselves in place (3M DP8005 data sheet). 4) Mixed acrylic gets hot as it cures, and the heat can deform thin or heat-sensitive parts, which matters for thin PLA walls (Loctite design guide, p. 12) ⚑ [uncertain]. Also, glue squeezed out into the air stays soft for longer, while glue inside the joint cures solid (Permabond TA4610 data sheet). 5) With the primer route, prime only the PE or PP part, let it dry (about 60 seconds for the kit; about 8-9 seconds for the industrial primer), then put one drop of superglue per square inch on the other part and press together for about 30 seconds (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet; Loctite SF 770 data sheet). Bond soon: the primer stays active for about 8 hours (Loctite SF 770 data sheet). 6) Load the joint in shear, not peel, especially with primed superglue (Loctite SF 770 data sheet); use wide overlapping or stepped joints. 7) Don't use it on nylon (PA) prints without testing, because 3M lists nylon as not recommended for DP8005 (3M DP8005 data sheet), although Permabond reports 5 MPa on PA6 with TA4610 (Permabond TA4610 data sheet). 8) For PP or PE prints, melting them together with a soldering iron or hot-air plastic welder is often simpler (Wikipedia 'Polyethylene'; Wikipedia 'Plastic welding'); see the Melted plastic weld entry.

### Buying one (off-the-shelf parts)

Search for 'polyolefin bonder', 'LSE structural adhesive', 'polypropylene adhesive' or 'structural plastic adhesive' (e.g. 3M Scotch-Weld DP8005 / DP8010, Loctite 3032, Permabond TA4610, brands) with the matching 'duo-pak applicator' or dispensing gun and 'static mixing nozzles'; 'polyolefin primer' or 'CA primer' (e.g. Loctite SF 770, brand) with a surface-insensitive cyanoacrylate; consumer 'plastics bonding system' kits with a primer pen; 'LSE double-sided tape' (e.g. 3M 300LSE or VHB LSE, brands) where tape will do; and a 'propane torch with flame spreader' for flame treatment (3M white paper; Loctite SF 770 data sheet; TAP Poly-Weld data sheet).

## Rules of thumb

- The lower a surface's energy (critical surface tension), the harder it is to glue: PTFE is about 18.5 mN/m, silicone 24, PE and PP 31 and polystyrene 33, while PVC (39), nylon 6,6 (43) and PET (43) are easier, and aluminium (about 500) and glass (about 1,000) are easy (3M 'Innovations in Bonding to Low Surface Energy Surfaces' white paper, table after Kinloch 1987).
- Water test: after flame treating PE (about five quick passes of 0.5 s each, 2-3 s in total, without melting it), wet it; if water beads up and runs off, the treatment didn't work; if it spreads out in a sheet, the surface is ready. Bond within 1 hour (TAP Poly-Weld data sheet).
- A polyolefin primer can raise superglue bond strength on hard-to-bond plastics by up to 20 times (Loctite 'Design Guide for Bonding Plastics', vol. 6, p. 6). The primer dries in about 8-9 seconds and stays active for about 8 hours (Loctite SF 770 data sheet).
- 3M DP8005: 10:1 mix, 2.5-3 minute work life, handling strength in 2-3 hours, full cure in 8-24 hours at 23 °C; bondline 0.005-0.008 in (0.13-0.2 mm); clamp at 4-8 psi for at least 2 hours (3M DP8005 data sheet).
- Permabond TA4610: 1:1 mix, gap fill up to 1 mm, pot life 5-8 minutes, fixture in 12-15 minutes, full cure in 24-36 hours at 23 °C (Permabond TA4610 data sheet).
- No-mix acrylics (resin on one face, activator on the other) cure only through about 0.76 mm from the activator face, so they need thin joints; static-mix acrylics cure through any depth but get hot (Loctite design guide, p. 12; the guide prints this as '0.30" (0.76 mm)', and the inch figure looks like a typo for 0.030") ⚑ [uncertain].
- A glue is called structural if it gives more than 1,000 psi (about 7 MPa) in an overlap shear test; LSE acrylics reach this on untreated PE and PP (3M white paper).

## Common failures

The glue peels cleanly off the PE or PP with no plastic torn off: the wrong glue was used, or the surface was dirty. Use an LSE product, clean with isopropyl alcohol, and remove mould release (Loctite design guide; 3M DP8005 data sheet). Weak or patchy bonds on plastics containing oils or anti-static additives (3M DP8005 data sheet). Bonds that never reach strength because the parts were joined after the few-minute work life, the cartridge was mixed by hand in the wrong ratio, or the glue layer was thinner than 0.13 mm (3M DP8005 data sheet). Primed superglue failing because too much primer was used, too long passed after priming, or the parts had gaps (Loctite SF 770 data sheet; Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet). Primed superglue joints breaking when peeled or flexed, or failing outdoors (Loctite SF 770 data sheet; Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet). Flame treatment that didn't work (water still beads up) or that melted and warped the part (TAP Poly-Weld data sheet). Glue squeezed out of the joint that stays tacky, because air slows the cure of acrylics (Permabond TA4610 data sheet). Joints that soften and [creep](../glossary.md#creep) when warm (3M DP8005 data sheet).

## Safety

Methacrylate glues smell strongly and are flammable (Permabond MMA article); methyl methacrylate irritates the eyes, skin and lungs and is a recognised skin sensitiser (contact allergen) (Wikipedia 'Methyl methacrylate'), so wear gloves and work with good ventilation. Primers and activators use highly flammable solvents (heptane or isopropyl acetate): keep them away from flames and sparks (Loctite SF 770 data sheet; Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet). Don't flame-treat in the same place you have just used primer or solvent. Superglue bonds skin in seconds and can cause burns on skin contact (Loctite Plastics Bonding System data sheet). Flame treatment uses an open propane flame: only touch the plastic with the tip of the outer flame for 2-3 seconds in total, and don't let it melt (TAP Poly-Weld data sheet). Large amounts of mixed static-mix acrylic get hot as they cure (Loctite design guide, p. 12).

## Related

- Cyanoacrylate (superglue): the same glue used in the primer route; without primer it gives only about 0.3-1 MPa on PE and PP (Loctite design guide)
- General-purpose structural acrylic (MMA) 'plastic welder' glues: tough and fast on ABS, PVC, polycarbonate, fibreglass and metal, but not on PE or PP (Devcon data sheet; Loctite design guide)
- Two-part epoxy: excellent on metal, wood and many plastics, but weak on untreated PE and PP (Loctite design guide)
- Melted plastic weld: hot-air, speed-tip or soldering-iron welding that melts PE or PP parts together with filler rod; often the strongest and cheapest fix for polyolefins (Wikipedia 'Plastic welding'; Wikipedia 'Polyethylene')
- Friction-welded plastic: melts a spinning rod of plastic into the seam with a rotary tool; another glue-free way to fuse plastic prints
- Solvent welding: fuses ABS, PVC and acrylic, but can't be used on PE or PP because they resist solvents (Wikipedia 'Polyolefin')
- Double-sided foam tape made for LSE plastics (e.g. 3M 300LSE or VHB LSE, brands): sticks to PP and TPO without primer; PE may still need flame, corona or plasma treatment (3M white paper)
- Polyolefin hot melts: hot-glue sticks formulated specially to stick to polyolefins (Loctite design guide)
- Screws, rivets, clips and snap fits: the traditional way to join PE and PP parts (3M white paper)
