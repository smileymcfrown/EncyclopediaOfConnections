Epoxy putty is a two-part dough of epoxy resin and hardener that you knead together by hand and press into place, where it sets into a hard, shrink-free, glued-on solid for filling gaps, plugging leaks, repairing and building up shapes that can then be sanded, drilled and tapped.

**In plain words:** A two-colour modelling clay: cut off equal amounts of the two parts, knead them together until the colour is even, press the dough into a crack, hole or joint, and within minutes to hours it sets rock hard and stuck in place; once hard you can sand, file, drill and even cut a screw thread in it. **Proper term:** Epoxy putty (two-part, hand-mixable epoxy putty).

**Also called:** epoxy putty stick, two-part epoxy putty, hand-mixable epoxy (Wikipedia; Loctite Repair Putty data sheet); repair putty, metal repair putty, steel-filled or steel-reinforced epoxy putty (Loctite EA 3463 data sheet; J-B Weld); epoxy modelling or sculpting putty; 'green stuff' among miniature modellers, from the yellow and blue parts that mix to green (Wikipedia); Milliput (brand; UK modelling and repair putty), Pratley Putty (brand; South Africa) (Wikipedia); J-B Weld SteelStik (brand), Loctite Repair Putty and Loctite EA 3463 (brands) (J-B Weld; Henkel data sheets); Oatey Fix-it Stick, QuikSteel, PC-7, KBS NuMetal, Gorilla Glue Epoxy Stick, POR 15 Epoxy Putty (brands tested by Project Farm; AllOutdoor).

## How it works

Each part is a clay-like paste: one holds epoxy resin, the other the hardener (for example a polymercaptan in Loctite Repair Putty), both packed with filler such as talc or steel powder (Loctite data sheets; Wikipedia). Kneading mixes them and starts a heat-releasing (exothermic) chemical reaction that joins the molecules into a hard, cross-linked plastic (a thermoset), so once set it will not melt when heated (Wikipedia). Because it hardens by reaction rather than by drying, it does not shrink, so it can fill gaps and be moulded into a solid part (Milliput data sheet; Loctite Repair Putty). It sticks to clean, roughened surfaces it is pressed against, and after it has set it can be sanded, filed, drilled and tapped like a soft metal or hard plastic (J-B Weld; Milliput on Wikipedia).

## Parts

Single piece once mixed: two doughs (resin and hardener), usually sold as two bars or as one stick with the hardener as a core or outer layer, mixed 1:1 (Wikipedia; Milliput instructions; Loctite Repair Putty). Helpers: gloves, a knife to cut it, water or a damp cloth to smooth it, sandpaper or a wire brush for preparing surfaces (Loctite Repair Putty data sheet).

## Everyday examples

- A plumber's repair stick pressed round a leaking pipe or tank (Loctite EA 3463; Loctite Repair Putty)
- Miniature and wargaming figures sculpted or converted with 'green stuff' (Wikipedia)
- A chipped porcelain figure or pottery restored with Milliput and painted to match (Milliput data sheet; Wikipedia)
- A stripped screw hole on a drawer pull filled and re-drilled; J-B Weld lists this among its home repair guides (J-B Weld)

## When to use it

Use it when you need to fill, rebuild or plug rather than just glue a thin line: filling holes, cracks and gaps; rebuilding a broken corner or missing piece; patching a leak in a pipe, tank or boat; restoring worn metal or rotten wood; and making a solid spot that can then be drilled and tapped for a screw (Loctite EA 3463; Loctite Repair Putty; J-B Weld; Milliput on Wikipedia). Its modelling-clay consistency lets it be shaped and formed before it [cures](../glossary.md#cure) (Loctite Repair Putty), and underwater-rated types handle damp or wet repairs (Loctite Repair Putty; Loctite EA 3463). For 3D prints it fills seams and gaps between glued parts, repairs broken prints and adds sculpted detail (Tinkerine video; Wikipedia).

## When not to use it

For thin, close-fitting glue joints use liquid epoxy or CA, which flow into a thin bond line; the putty's own lap-shear strength is modest (3.8 MPa for Loctite EA 3463 on steel; Henkel). Don't use it for structural or load-bearing joints, parts that flex or move, or large or deep holes (Loctite Repair Putty). Avoid it on polyethylene, polypropylene, non-stick (PTFE) surfaces, nylon and flexible materials; use a low-surface-energy adhesive, plastic welding (see Melted plastic weld) or mechanical fasteners there (Loctite Repair Putty). Avoid it for hot parts beyond its rating (e.g. above 70 °C for load-bearing Loctite Repair Putty bonds) and in pure-oxygen systems (Henkel). If the parts must come apart later, use screws, threaded inserts or a removable joint. For a smooth cosmetic finish over large areas of a print, spray filler or body filler sands more easily (Prusa post-processing guide).

## Details

- **What it does (jobs):**
  - glue or bond: sticks broken parts together and bonds itself to the surface it is pressed onto (Loctite Repair Putty)
  - seal it, or pass cables or fluid through: plugs leaks in pipes and tanks and cures under water (Loctite EA 3463; Loctite Repair Putty)
  - join and hold: fills oversized or stripped holes so a screw can be fitted again, and builds up worn or missing material (Loctite EA 3463; J-B Weld)
- **Movement:** none: a rigid repair or joint
- **Directions it can move (degrees of freedom):** 0: the bonded or filled parts are locked together
- **Special behaviour:**
  - none of the listed behaviours. Its own tricks: it fills gaps and can be modelled into missing pieces without shrinking; once hard it can be machined (sanded, drilled, tapped); many repair putties cure on damp surfaces and even under water (Milliput data sheet; J-B Weld; Loctite EA 3463; Loctite Repair Putty)
- **How it holds:**
  - [material bond](../glossary.md#form-fit--force-fit--material-bond) (glue, solvent or melting): the cured epoxy sticks to the surfaces it was pressed onto (Loctite Repair Putty)
  - shape (the parts interlock; form-fit): putty pressed into holes, cracks and [undercuts](../glossary.md#undercut) also locks in mechanically once hard, which is why roughening the surface improves adhesion (Milliput; Loctite) ⚑ [uncertain]
- **Input and output:** not applicable
- **Power:** passive (no power needed): cures by itself at room temperature once mixed; gentle warmth speeds the cure and cold slows it (Loctite EA 3463 cure-speed chart; Milliput data sheet).
- **Load:** medium, best in compression and as a filler: a steel-filled putty (Loctite EA 3463) has 50 MPa (7,200 psi) compressive strength and 18.8 MPa (2,700 psi) tensile strength, but only 3.8 MPa (550 psi) lap-shear strength on grit-blasted steel and 0.3% elongation, so it is hard and [brittle](../glossary.md#brittle--ductile). Loctite Repair Putty All-Purpose gives about 1,700 psi (11.7 MPa) [shear](../glossary.md#peel-vs-shear) on steel and about 970 psi (6.7 MPa) on beech after 24 hours, much less on wet surfaces (Henkel data sheets). J-B Weld rates SteelStik at 4,000 psi (test not stated) (J-B Weld). Wikipedia notes that 'hard as steel' claims refer to surface hardness (Shore rating), and the putties are much weaker than steel in tension and shear. Loctite says not to use its repair putty for structural applications or for repairs subject to movement.
- **Play and precision:** not applicable (nothing moves). It fills gaps of any practical size, but Loctite advises against using its repair putty for large or deep holes (Loctite Repair Putty).
- **Taking it apart:** permanent: cured putty can only be cut away with a sharp blade, ground or drilled out (Loctite Repair Putty: 'Cured putty may be cut away with caution using a sharp blade').
- **How often it can be reused:** once: it cannot be softened and reused; a failed repair is cut or ground out and redone
- **Sealing:** watertight: it forms a waterproof seal and is used to stop leaks in pipes, tanks, tubs and showers; Loctite's all-purpose putty is rated safe for drinking water and cures under water (Loctite Repair Putty; Loctite EA 3463). Some putties held pipe pressure in Project Farm's test (PC-7 paste did best) (AllOutdoor).
- **Adjustable:** no once set. [Working time](../glossary.md#pot-life--working-time) depends on the type: fast metal-repair sticks give only 3-5 minutes (Loctite Repair Putty; Loctite EA 3463: 4 minutes), while modelling putty such as Milliput is sticky at first, turns rubbery and less tacky after about an hour, and is hard in 3-4 hours; one sculptor gives it about two hours of malleable time (Milliput data sheet). Mixed Milliput can be paused for up to 36 hours by putting it in a deep freezer (Milliput instructions).
- **Access needed:** one side only (blind): putty is pressed into a crack, hole or leak from the side you can reach, or packed around a joint (Loctite Repair Putty: 'Work putty firmly into the crack or hole'; Loctite EA 3463).
- **Materials:** Made of epoxy resin and hardener with fillers such as talc or steel powder (Loctite data sheets). Bonds: metals (iron, steel, aluminium), wood, ceramic and porcelain, glass, brick, concrete, fibreglass, PVC and most rigid and semi-flexible plastics (Loctite Repair Putty; Milliput on Wikipedia). Not recommended: polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP), non-stick coatings (PTFE), nylon and flexible materials (Loctite Repair Putty). For 3D prints this suggests rigid PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA and resin parts are fine after sanding, while nylon, PP and TPU prints are poor candidates; no maker test data on PLA or PETG were found ⚑ [uncertain]. Grades exist for metal repair (steel-filled), high temperatures, tanks, plumbing and drinking water, and fine modelling (J-B Weld; Henkel data sheets; Milliput on Wikipedia).
- **Heat, cold, water and wear:** Heat: it will not melt once cured (Wikipedia), but it softens and weakens: Loctite Repair Putty is rated to 121 °C for fills and 70 °C for load-bearing bonds, Loctite EA 3463 has a glass transition temperature of 54 °C, and J-B Weld SteelStik claims 177 °C (350 °F) (Henkel; J-B Weld). Cold: Loctite Repair Putty is rated down to -27 °C, and the uncured putty is not damaged by freezing (Henkel). Water: waterproof and resistant to most common solvents once cured, and some grades cure under water, but bonds made on wet surfaces are weaker (Loctite Repair Putty; Loctite EA 3463). Oil: J-B Weld lists SteelStik as water and oil resistant. Flexing and vibration: very little stretch (0.3% elongation for EA 3463), so it cracks if the part bends or moves (Henkel). Long-term [creep](../glossary.md#creep) and UV behaviour were not found in the sources opened ⚑ [uncertain].

## Making it

- **Ways to make or get it:**
  - off-the-shelf part: sticks and bars of general-purpose, metal-filled, wood, plumbing and modelling putty
  - 3D print (FDM): filling gaps and seams in printed parts and repairing broken prints before sanding and painting (Tinkerine video)
  - 3D print (resin): repairing and filling resin prints (rigid plastic) ⚑ [uncertain]
  - woodwork: filling holes and rotted areas and rebuilding wood; Loctite quotes bond strength on beech (Loctite Repair Putty; Milliput on Wikipedia)
  - metal repair and CNC-style finishing: rebuilding worn metal parts, filling oversized bolt holes, then drilling, tapping, filing or machining (Loctite EA 3463; J-B Weld)
  - model making and sculpture: miniatures ('green stuff'), restoring porcelain and pottery (Wikipedia; Milliput data sheet)
- **Printing it in one piece (print in place):** no: it is a separate material pressed onto or into parts after printing, although a print can be designed with a pocket for it.
- **Tools:** A knife to cut off the amount needed; nitrile or other impervious gloves for kneading; water or a damp cloth for smoothing; sandpaper or a wire brush and a solvent (or soap and warm water) to clean and roughen the surfaces first. After curing: files, sandpaper, a drill, and a tap if you want a thread (Loctite Repair Putty; Loctite EA 3463; Milliput instructions; J-B Weld).
- **Difficulty:** easy: no measuring or special tools, just knead equal parts to an even colour and press in place; the main skills are thorough mixing, clean roughened surfaces and working within the pot life (Loctite Repair Putty; Milliput instructions).

### 3D printing tips

1) Prepare the print: sand the area to remove the shiny skin and give the putty something to grip, then clean off dust and grease; 'abrading the surface will improve adhesion' (Milliput instructions; Loctite Repair Putty). 2) Mix equal parts until there are no streaks at all: about 1 minute for Loctite Repair Putty, 2-3 minutes for Loctite EA 3463, at least 5 minutes plus one more for Milliput (Henkel data sheets; Milliput instructions). Wear gloves. 3) Press it firmly into the gap so it contacts all surfaces, then smooth it with a wet finger or damp cloth before it hardens; water stops it sticking to fingers and tools (Milliput; Loctite). 4) Choose the working time to suit the job: fast metal putties (3-5 minutes) for small repairs, modelling putty (roughly 1-2 hours before it gets too firm) for sculpting details or filling seams on cosplay and model prints (Loctite Repair Putty; Milliput data sheet). 5) To make a strong screw hole in a print, fill an oversized hole or pocket with putty, let it cure fully (J-B Weld SteelStik can be drilled and tapped after 60 minutes; Loctite Repair Putty after 15-60 minutes), then drill a tap-size hole and cut the thread; Loctite EA 3463 is sold for 'filling oversized bolt holes' (J-B Weld; Henkel data sheets). In Project Farm's drill-and-tap pull-out test KBS NuMetal did best, then J-B Weld SteelStik and Oatey Pro Poxy 20 (AllOutdoor). 6) Design the print with a recess, holes or undercuts where the putty goes, so it locks in by shape as well as by glue ⚑ [uncertain]. 7) Keep thick blobs modest on thin PLA parts: the curing reaction gives off heat (Wikipedia) and PLA softens at low temperatures ⚑ [uncertain]. 8) Avoid it on nylon, PP and flexible TPU prints (Loctite Repair Putty). 9) Sand the cured putty flush with P100-P400 paper, wearing a dust mask ('Do not breathe fumes/dust while sanding', Loctite Repair Putty), then prime and paint as for body filler (Prusa post-processing guide).

### Buying one (off-the-shelf parts)

Search for 'epoxy putty stick' (co-extruded, cut-and-knead); 'steel-reinforced' or 'steel-filled epoxy putty' (e.g. J-B Weld SteelStik, Loctite EA 3463, QuikSteel, brands); 'high-temperature epoxy putty' (J-B Weld HighHeat, brand); 'plumbing' or 'potable-water epoxy putty' and 'underwater epoxy putty'; 'tank repair putty' (J-B Weld TankWeld, brand); 'wood epoxy putty'; and 'epoxy modelling putty' or 'sculpting putty' (Milliput grades including Superfine White, brand; 'green stuff') (J-B Weld; Henkel; Wikipedia; Milliput data sheet).

## Rules of thumb

- Mix equal amounts and knead until the colour is completely even with no streaks: at least 5 minutes plus one more for Milliput, 2-3 minutes for Loctite EA 3463, about 1 minute for Loctite Repair Putty (Milliput instructions; Henkel data sheets).
- Working time: 3-5 minutes for fast repair sticks (Loctite Repair Putty; Loctite EA 3463: gel 3 min, working life 4 min at 25 °C); modelling putty such as Milliput turns rubbery after about an hour, hard and tack-free in 3-4 hours and rock hard overnight (Milliput data sheet).
- Machining: J-B Weld SteelStik 'sets in 5 minutes and after 60 minutes, can be drilled, tapped, machined, ground, filed and painted' (J-B Weld); Loctite Repair Putty can be sanded, drilled or painted after 15-60 minutes and fully cures in 24 hours (Henkel); Milliput hardens fully in 24 hours before carving, sanding and drilling (Wikipedia, Milliput).
- Strength (steel-filled Loctite EA 3463, cured 1 week at 23 °C): compressive 50 MPa, tensile 18.8 MPa, lap shear 3.8 MPa on grit-blasted mild steel, Shore D 70, elongation 0.3% (Henkel TDS, March 2021). Loctite Repair Putty All-Purpose: shear 1,703 ± 58 psi on steel and 969 ± 26 psi on beech, falling to 747 and 207 psi on wet surfaces; Shore D 84 (Henkel TDS, 2019).
- Temperature: Loctite Repair Putty is rated -27 to 121 °C for fills but only up to 70 °C for load-bearing bonds; Loctite EA 3463's glass transition (softening) temperature is 54 °C; J-B Weld SteelStik 'will withstand temperatures up to 350°F' (177 °C) (Henkel; J-B Weld).
- Apply between about 4 and 35 °C (Loctite Repair Putty) or 15-30 °C (Loctite EA 3463), and never to surfaces hotter than 66 °C (Loctite EA 3463). Store unopened sticks at about 8-21 °C (Loctite EA 3463).
- Shelf life about 2 years (Milliput: 'about 2 years', often workable much longer; Loctite Repair Putty: 24 months from manufacture).
- Surface preparation matters most: clean, dry, grease-free and roughened; bonding to wet surfaces works but is weaker (Henkel data sheets; Milliput instructions).

## Common failures

Soft, sticky or weak spots from uneven mixing: knead until there are no streaks, especially with same-colour grades such as Superfine White Milliput (Milliput instructions). The repair pops off because the surface was greasy, dusty, smooth or wet: clean, dry and roughen first (Henkel; Milliput). It won't stick to PE, PP, PTFE or nylon (Loctite Repair Putty). Cracks where the part flexes or moves, because cured putty barely stretches (Loctite Repair Putty; EA 3463 elongation 0.3%). Working past the pot life, so it no longer wets the surface; mix only small amounts of fast putties (Henkel). Weak threads or loose screws when drilled and tapped before full cure; wait the full time (J-B Weld; Henkel). Softening near hot parts above its rated temperature (Henkel). Hardened putty stuck on tools: clean them straight away with a wet rag (Milliput).

## Safety

Uncured epoxy can cause allergic skin reactions and irritate eyes, skin and airways: wear impervious (e.g. nitrile) gloves and wash hands after use (Loctite Repair Putty; Milliput instructions). Don't lick fingers or tools to smooth it, a habit among sculptors that the Milliput data sheet calls 'inadvisable on health grounds'; use water or a lubricant instead. Don't breathe the dust when sanding cured putty (Loctite Repair Putty). Loctite's label carries a California Proposition 65 cancer warning and 'may cause allergic skin reaction'. The mixing reaction gives off heat (Wikipedia), so a large lump can get warm ⚑ [uncertain]. Keep out of reach of children, and don't use steel-filled putty in pure-oxygen or chlorine systems (Henkel; Milliput).

## Related

- Two-part epoxy (liquid): the same chemistry as a liquid; better for thin, close-fitting bond lines and higher shear strength, but runs and needs containment for filling
- Polyester body filler and spray filler: cheaper, sand more easily for cosmetic smoothing of prints, but not for strong repairs or leaks (Prusa post-processing guide)
- Cyanoacrylate mixed with talc, microballoons or baking soda: a quick 'makeshift putty' for small gaps in prints (Prusa gluing guide)
- Heat-set threaded inserts and printed or tapped threads: the usual way to put a screw thread into a print; putty is an alternative for repairing stripped or oversized holes (CNC Kitchen tests via Hackaday)
- Hot-melt adhesive (glue gun): also fills gaps quickly, but stays soft, weak and heat-sensitive
- Structural acrylic and low-surface-energy plastic adhesives: for PE and PP, which epoxy putty cannot bond (Loctite Repair Putty limitation)
- Silicone and MS-polymer sealants: flexible, waterproof gap-fillers where the joint moves; epoxy putty is rigid
- Melted plastic weld: for a watertight printed tank, makers suggest welding the seams first (e.g. with a soldering iron) and then sealing, using 'glue and sealer' rather than one product alone (r/3Dprinting thread read through the Arctic Shift archive)
