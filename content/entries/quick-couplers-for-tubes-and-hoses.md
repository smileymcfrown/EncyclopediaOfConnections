A tube fitting that locks a plastic or soft-metal tube in place and seals it as soon as the tube is pushed in, and releases it by hand when you push its release collar in, or (for hose couplers) a pair of plug-and-socket halves that snap together and come apart when you slide back a spring-loaded sleeve.

**In plain words:** A fitting you just push a tube into: hidden teeth grab the tube and a rubber ring seals it, and it only lets go when you press a collar in (or, on hose couplers, pull a sleeve back). **Proper term:** Push-to-connect fitting (push-in or push-fit tube fitting) and quick-disconnect coupling (ball-latching, sleeve-type quick coupler).

**Also called:** push-in fitting, push-fit fitting, push-to-connect (PTC) fitting, instant fitting (Bearing Boys); quick connect fitting, push fitting, quick connect coupling (Wikipedia, Quick connect fitting); one-touch fitting (SMC's (brand) catalogue name); push-quick fitting (Clippard's (brand) name); John Guest (brand) or Speedfit (brand) fitting, SharkBite (brand) fitting (plumbing and water filters; Printables 198167 names SharkBite); 3D printing: pneumatic fitting, Bowden coupler, PC4-M6 / PC4-M10 fitting, 'Festo fitting' (Festo (brand); Prusa users' name); quick disconnect (QD), quick coupler, quick-release coupling, air coupler, air-line fitting, pneumatic coupling (Wikipedia, Air-line fitting; Midstate); ball-latching or snap-type coupler; the two halves are the male nipple or plug and the female coupler or socket (Midstate; Wikipedia, Air-line fitting); click-on garden hose connector, Hozelock (brand) or Gardena (brand) connector (Wikipedia, Hose coupling); cam-and-groove coupling, camlock (cam lock) fitting, Type A to F camlocks (letter codes for the male adapter and female coupler ends) (Wikipedia 'Cam and groove'). Name clash: on Printables, 'CAMLOCK' also names a 3D-printed vacuum-hose (dust-extraction) connector system, not this coupling (Printables models 493835 and 624672, read through the Printables API); thumb-latch coupling, plastic quick-disconnect coupling, valved (shut-off) coupling, multi-lumen (twin-tube) coupling; THOMAFLUID (brand) (RCT catalogue page); CPC or Colder (brand) coupling: thumb-latch plastic couplings, with or without valves, from Colder Products Company (Fresh Water Systems CPC page).

## How it works

Push-to-connect: the tube slides through a ring of angled metal teeth (the [collet](../glossary.md#collet)) and an [O-ring](../glossary.md#o-ring) until it hits a stop; if the tube is pulled, or pressure pushes it outward, the collet is dragged into a tapered groove that squeezes its teeth into the tube, so it grips harder the more you pull, while the O-ring seals round the outside of the tube (Wikipedia, Quick connect fitting; Fresh Water Systems). Pressing the collar squarely into the body spreads the teeth off the tube so it can be pulled out (John Guest via US Plastic). Sleeve couplers: when the plug is pushed in, spring-loaded balls in the socket drop into a groove on the plug and the sleeve slides over them to trap them; pulling the sleeve back gives the balls room to move out and frees the plug (Wikipedia, Air-line fitting; Midstate). Garden-hose click connectors work the same way, with a spring-loaded outer barrel that locks onto the male end (Wikipedia, Hose coupling). Cam-and-groove (camlock) couplings, used on larger hoses, have two cam levers on the female coupler: swinging them shut pulls the grooved male adapter into the socket against a gasket, and the levers lock by [over-centre](../glossary.md#over-centre) geometry so they can't open by accident; because the groove runs all the way round, no lining up is needed and the gasket is squeezed the same amount every time (Wikipedia 'Cam and groove'). Small plastic thumb-latch couplings for lab and analyser tubing lock by themselves when the nipple is plugged in and come apart after 'a short thumb pressure on the lock', all with one hand; the nipple seals with an O-ring, and either half can have a shut-off valve so the line stays closed when parted (RCT catalogue page).

## Parts

A small assembly that acts as one unit. Push-to-connect fitting: body (often with a threaded stud for a port), collet or gripper ring with stainless-steel teeth, release collar or button, O-ring seal, and a tube stop inside; an optional plastic locking clip stops the collar being pushed in (Clippard; John Guest via US Plastic; Fresh Water Systems). Sleeve-type quick coupler: a female coupler (socket) holding a ring of steel balls, a spring-loaded sliding sleeve, seals and often a shut-off valve, plus a separate male nipple (plug) with a groove round it (Wikipedia, Air-line fitting; Midstate).

## Everyday examples

- The push-in fittings that hold the PTFE tube on Bowden-type 3D printers, often with a small blue collet clip under the release collar (Printables 135121; Printables 529258)
- Plastic tubing on under-sink water filters and reverse-osmosis units (Fresh Water Systems; Printables 198167)
- Click-on garden hose connectors (Hozelock (brand) or Gardena (brand) style) (Wikipedia, Hose coupling)
- Workshop compressed-air hoses snapped onto air tools and tyre inflators with a pull-back-sleeve coupler (Wikipedia, Air-line fitting; Midstate)

## When to use it

Use a push-to-connect fitting when you need to join firm plastic tubing (nylon, polyurethane, polyethylene, PTFE) quickly and neatly for compressed air, vacuum, water or other low-to-medium pressure fluids, and you may want to take it apart again: pneumatic models and robots, water filters and cooling, aquarium and hydroponic setups, and 3D-printer Bowden and filament-guide tubes. It connects with one push and no tools, gives high flow because nothing narrows the bore, and suits frequent reconnection better than a barb (Clippard). Use a sleeve-type quick coupler (plug and socket) when a hose must be connected and unplugged often, one-handed, e.g. swapping air tools, and choose shut-off (valved) couplings so the line doesn't empty when parted (Midstate; Wikipedia, Quick connect fitting). Choose a cam-and-groove (camlock) coupling for large liquid hoses that are swapped often in dirty conditions where threads would clog, such as pump-truck and tanker hoses or drum filling, since it connects without tools and can't be over- or under-tightened (Wikipedia 'Cam and groove'); choose small plastic thumb-latch couplings for lab, analyser and other small-bore tubing that has to be plugged and unplugged one-handed (RCT catalogue page; Fresh Water Systems CPC page).

## When not to use it

Don't use push-in fittings with soft, stretchy tubing (silicone, soft PVC): use a hose barb with a clamp, or add a tube insert (Clippard; John Guest via US Plastic). Don't use them where the tube rotates, swings, or travels with a moving part: use rotary fittings, coiled tubing, or a cable carrier (SMC KQG2 manual). Avoid heavy vibration or shock, strong side loads, hard metal tubes, very high pressures and pressure spikes: use compression, threaded or crimped fittings (SMC KQG2 manual; John Guest via US Plastic). If you want the cheapest, most leak-free joint that you never undo, a barb is better (Clippard). Don't rely on a 3D-printed push-in fitting for real compressed-air pressure; printed ones are experimental (Printables 1094373). Don't use cam-and-groove couplings for compressed air, steam or other gases (Wikipedia 'Cam and groove'); use an air coupler rated for gas.

## Details

- **What it does (jobs):**
  - seal it, or pass cables or fluid through: joins a tube or hose so air, water or other fluid passes without leaking
  - join and hold: holds the tube in against pulling and internal pressure
  - lock, and release by hand: pressing the collar or pulling the sleeve undoes it with no tools
- **Movement:** none: the tube or plug is held still once connected. Plumbing push-fit joints stay rotatable after assembly (Wikipedia 'Piping and plumbing fitting'), and elbow and branch versions can be turned to point the right way after connecting but must not be used while turning; for tubes that keep rotating or swinging, rotary (swivel) fittings are used instead (SMC KQG2 manual)
- **Directions it can move (degrees of freedom):** 0: the tube is locked in place along and around its axis in normal use; the tube moves back slightly when first pulled, as the teeth bite (Clippard: pull the tubing back gently until the gripper ring grips)
- **Special behaviour:**
  - quick-release (tool-free): press the collar or button in (push-to-connect) or pull the sleeve back (couplers) and the tube or plug comes out (John Guest via US Plastic; Midstate)
  - hand-release only (locks under load, releases when a person operates it): pulling on the tube or pressure inside only makes the teeth bite harder; it lets go only when the collar is pushed in (Wikipedia, Quick connect fitting; Fresh Water Systems)
  - one-way (moves freely one way, locks the other): the tube slides in easily past the angled teeth but cannot slide back out
  - [fail-safe](../glossary.md#fail-safe--fail-secure) or breakaway: only special breakaway couplings, which part on purpose if the hose is pulled too hard (e.g. a truck driving off while refuelling) and seal themselves to prevent a spill (Midstate)
- **How it holds:**
  - friction or clamping ([force-fit](../glossary.md#form-fit--force-fit--material-bond)): the collet is wedged into a [taper](../glossary.md#taper) so its teeth squeeze and bite into the tube (Fresh Water Systems)
  - shape (the parts interlock; form-fit): the teeth dig into the tube surface; in sleeve couplers the balls sit in a groove on the plug (Wikipedia, Air-line fitting)
  - spring force (a spring or springy part presses it in place): the springy collet fingers and the spring behind the coupler sleeve keep it engaged (Midstate)
- **Input and output:** not applicable: it joins tubes and passes fluid, not motion
- **Power:** passive (no power needed)
- **Load:** medium for its size: holds tube pull-out of more than 20 lb (about 9 kg) when not pressurised in a small plastic fitting (Clippard) and full rated pressure. Strong against pulling straight out; weak against side loads, bending close to the fitting, twisting, rotation, vibration and impact, which can crack the fitting or release or burst the tube (SMC KQG2 manual; Fresh Water Systems)
- **Play and precision:** small: the tube shifts back slightly as the teeth bite (Clippard). It tolerates almost no [misalignment](../glossary.md#misalignment): push the tube in straight (within about 0-5 degrees) and keep side loads off it (SMC KQG2 manual; Fresh Water Systems). It is not used to locate parts
- **Taking it apart:** tool-free quick release: press the release collar in and pull the tube out, or pull back the coupler sleeve; the system must be depressurised first (John Guest via US Plastic; SMC KQG2 manual)
- **How often it can be reused:** many times: the fitting can be re-used (John Guest via US Plastic), but the teeth mark the tube, so cut off the marked end before pushing a used tube back in (SMC KQG2 manual; Clippard); frequent removal gradually scratches the tube until it leaks past the O-ring (Fresh Water Systems). Sleeve-type quick couplers are made for frequent connecting and disconnecting (Midstate); plumbing push-fit ('push-to-pull') fittings 'can easily be removed and re-used' (Wikipedia 'Piping and plumbing fitting')
- **Sealing:** airtight or pressure: an O-ring inside the fitting seals round the outside of the tube (Wikipedia, Quick connect fitting). Example ratings: small plastic push-in fittings 0-150 psig and vacuum to 29.5 inHg (Clippard); stainless push-in fittings -100 kPa to 1 MPa, but not for holding a vacuum in a leak tester because they leak slightly (SMC KQG2 manual). Quick couplers seal with O-rings or a compressed tip, and valved (shut-off) couplers close off the flow when parted (Wikipedia, Air-line fitting; Midstate)
- **Adjustable:** no: the grip and seal are set by the fitting and the tube size; elbow fittings can only be swivelled to aim them (SMC KQG2 manual)
- **Access needed:** end of a tube or shaft: you need the cut end of the tube and room to push it straight in and to press the release collar with a finger or tool; the fitting body is either inline or screwed into a port
- **Materials:** Fittings: plastic body with nickel-plated brass stud, stainless-steel gripper ring and Buna-N (nitrile) seals (Clippard); all-stainless 316 versions with FKM seals for steam (SMC KQG2 manual); quick couplers in carbon steel, brass or stainless (Midstate) or plastics such as acetal, polypropylene, nylon and PEEK (Wikipedia, Quick connect fitting). Tubes: nylon, polyurethane, polyethylene or polypropylene (Clippard); soft copper, brass or mild steel tube if within [tolerance](../glossary.md#tolerance), but not hard metal tube (John Guest via US Plastic); fluoropolymer (FEP, PFA, PTFE) tube with an inner sleeve where temperatures swing (SMC KQG2 manual). Soft or thin-walled tubing needs a tube insert (John Guest via US Plastic). 3D printers use 4 mm PTFE tube in PC4 fittings (Printables 529258). Cam-and-groove couplings: stainless steel, aluminium, brass or polypropylene (Wikipedia 'Cam and groove'); plastic thumb-latch couplings: acetal (POM) bodies with nitrile (NBR) seals (RCT catalogue page)
- **Heat, cold, water and wear:** Heat and cold: small plastic push-in fittings are rated 32-140 °F (0-60 °C) (Clippard); stainless versions -5 to 150 °C, no freezing (SMC KQG2 manual). Below about 5 °C, water in the line can freeze and damage seals (SMC). Warmer water lowers the pressure rating (Fresh Water Systems). Polyurethane tube swells with pressure and heat, so a used tube may no longer fit back in (SMC). Sunlight, vibration and chemicals: avoid long direct sunlight, heavy vibration or shock, radiant heat, corrosive gases, sea water, and cutting oils or coolants unless the fitting is rated for them (SMC KQG2 manual). Ageing: after many years the O-ring can dry out and drip (Fresh Water Systems). Printed parts: PLA starts to give at about 60 °C and PETG at about 80 °C (CNC Kitchen).

## Making it

- **Ways to make or get it:**
  - off-the-shelf part: push-to-connect straights, elbows, tees, bulkhead and threaded-stud fittings; quick-disconnect coupler and plug pairs; garden-hose click connectors
  - 3D print (FDM): housings, pass-throughs and adapters that hold a bought fitting (e.g. PC4-M10 threads), collet locking clips, release tools, blanking plugs, and experimental printed push-to-connect fittings and garden-hose connectors (Printables 1094373, 1314393, 1255364)
  - 3D print (resin): a printed female garden-hose connector was made in ABS-like resin (Printables 1314393) ⚑ [uncertain: one example only]
  - CNC: not typical for makers; commercial metal bodies are machined or forged (Midstate) ⚑ [uncertain]
- **Printing it in one piece (print in place):** partly: one experimental push-to-connect fitting is designed to print in place and grips the tube, but needs a bought O-ring to hold pressure (Printables 1094373). Most printed designs are separate parts: printed garden-hose couplings need an O-ring (Printables 1255364) and usually a spring (a springless lockable remix exists, Printables 1314393); most makers simply screw a bought fitting into a printed part
- **Tools:** None to connect or release (a small printed or bought release tool helps with tiny collets; Printables 198167, 285470). A proper tube cutter for a square cut; don't use pliers, nippers or scissors, which squash the tube (SMC KQG2 manual). A spanner to screw a threaded fitting into its port (Clippard; SMC)
- **Difficulty:** easy to fit, hard to make: buy one and print the housing or adapter around it; the hardened teeth and accurate O-ring groove are hard to print, and printed versions are experimental (Printables 1094373 is 'work in progress'; Printables 1572668 warns printed collars hold the tube less well than ones with metal teeth)

### 3D printing tips

1) Best approach: design a printed part that holds a bought fitting (a threaded hole or a [heat-set insert](../glossary.md#heat-set-insert) for the fitting's M5, M6 or M10 stud, or a clamp), and let the metal-toothed collet do the gripping; one printed PTFE tube joiner comes with printed collars but its designer recommends bought 4 mm collars with metal teeth because printed ones hold less well (Printables 1572668). 2) Threads and plugs for fittings are small: makers print them with fine layers (0.1-0.12 mm) and 100% [infill](../glossary.md#infill) (Printables 233088; Printables 263357, which also recommends a brim). 3) A printed PC4-M10 substitute clamp was printed in PETG for flexibility and the PLA nut kept rigid; the 4 mm hole may need drilling out to fit the tube (Printables 529258). 4) Printed garden-hose connectors: print the gripping fingers facing down with a brim, use PETG outdoors, and fit an O-ring (bought, or printed in TPU); the fingers are fragile (Printables 1255364); orient the part so the O-ring seat is smooth (Printables 1281989). 5) Sliding sleeves, collars and latches need [clearance](../glossary.md#clearance) to move: start at about 0.3 mm for moving parts (Prusa Knowledge Base) and tune for your printer. 6) Plastic springs that stay loaded [creep](../glossary.md#creep) and relax, so use a steel spring behind a printed sleeve or latch (DuPont design guide, p. 36). 7) Keep printed fittings for low pressure and room temperature: PLA starts to give at about 60 °C and PETG at about 80 °C (CNC Kitchen); an experimental printed push-to-connect fitting is 'not as springy as metal versions' (Printables 1094373). 8) For 3D-printer Bowden tubes, a printed collet clip that slides under the collar stops the collar being pushed in and removes play on retraction (Printables 135121, which halved the retraction distance needed from 4 mm to 2 mm).

### Buying one (off-the-shelf parts)

Search for: 'push-to-connect fitting', 'push-in fitting' or 'instant fitting' (straight union, elbow, tee, Y, bulkhead union, male stud with M5, 1/8 NPT or R1/8 thread, reducer, plug); 'one-touch fitting' (SMC (brand) naming); 'push-quick fitting' (Clippard (brand)); 'rotary one-touch fitting' for tubes that turn (SMC); 'PC4-M6' or 'PC4-M10 pneumatic fitting' and 'collet clip' for 3D-printer Bowden tubes; 'release tool' for small collets; 'tube insert' or 'tube stiffener' for soft tubing (John Guest via US Plastic); 'inner sleeve' for fluoropolymer tube (SMC); 'tube cutter' (e.g. SMC TK-1). Quick couplers: 'quick-disconnect coupling', 'quick coupler' and matching 'nipple' or 'plug' in a named profile such as 'Industrial 1/4 in' (US), 'ARO 210', 'Tru-Flate/Automotive', 'Euro 7.2 (high-flow)', 'Nitto', or 'Rectus 25' (European) (Wikipedia, Air-line fitting); 'ISO 4414 safety coupling'; 'single shut-off' or 'double shut-off'; 'flat-face coupling' (hydraulic) (Midstate). Garden: 'hose quick connector' or 'click-on hose connector' (Hozelock (brand), Gardena (brand)). Tubing: '95A polyurethane tubing', 'nylon tubing', 'polyethylene tubing' by outside diameter (4, 6, 8 mm; 5/32, 1/4, 3/8 in).

## Rules of thumb

- Push-in fittings are sized by the tube's OUTSIDE diameter, hose barbs by the tube's INSIDE diameter (Clippard, Barb Fittings vs. Push-Quick Fittings).
- Use firm tubing: Clippard recommends 95A polyurethane for push-quick fittings (85A for barbs) and lists minimum hardness Shore A85 for polyurethane and Shore D44 for polyethylene, polypropylene and nylon (Clippard wiki; Clippard Push-Quick datasheet, p. 290).
- Tube outside diameter must be accurate: +/-0.005 in (about 0.13 mm) for polyurethane and +/-0.004 in (about 0.1 mm) for polyethylene, polypropylene and nylon (Clippard Push-Quick datasheet, p. 290); John Guest lists +0.001/-0.003 in for 5/32-3/16 in tube and +0.001/-0.004 in for 1/4-1/2 in tube (John Guest via US Plastic).
- Example ratings for small plastic push-in fittings: 0-150 psig at 130 °F, vacuum to 29.5 inHg, 32-140 °F, tube pull-out force over 20 lb unpressurised, burst 350 psig at 75 °F (Clippard Push-Quick datasheet, p. 290). Stainless 316 push-in fittings: -100 kPa to 1 MPa, proof pressure 3.0 MPa, -5 to 150 °C with no freezing (SMC KQG2 manual).
- Cut the tube square with a tube cutter, not pliers, nippers or scissors (SMC KQG2 manual); the outside must be free of score marks and burrs (John Guest via US Plastic).
- Push the tube in straight (within about 0-5 degrees) until it stops, then pull it back gently to check it has gripped (SMC KQG2 manual; Clippard). The fitting grips before it seals, so a half-inserted tube can hold but still leak (John Guest via US Plastic).
- Mark the insertion depth on the tube so you can see it has gone all the way in; Fresh Water Systems suggests a line about 3/4 in from the end for its water-filter fittings (the depth depends on the fitting size; SMC gives it as dimension 'M' in each drawing).
- Keep the tube's bend near the fitting no tighter than the tube's minimum bending radius, and leave some slack for pressure and length changes (SMC KQG2 manual).
- Use a tube insert in soft or thin-walled tubing (John Guest via US Plastic), and an inner sleeve in fluoropolymer tubing where the fluid temperature swings a lot (SMC KQG2 manual).
- To re-use a tube, cut off the part that was inside the fitting first (SMC KQG2 manual; Clippard Push-Quick datasheet).
- Keep surge (pressure spike) pressure below the maximum working pressure, or the tube may be pushed out (SMC KQG2 manual).
- Quick-coupler plugs and sockets must share the same profile; mismatched pairs may still latch but seal poorly (Wikipedia, Air-line fitting).
- Printed moving parts (sleeves, collars, latches): start with at least 0.3 mm clearance (Prusa Knowledge Base, Modeling with 3D printing in mind).
- Plumbing push-fit: cut the pipe end square so it sits against the stop, and cleanly so it doesn't damage the O-ring as it goes in (Wikipedia 'Piping and plumbing fitting').
- Example rating for a small twin-tube plastic thumb-latch coupling: -40 to +80 °C and up to 6.9 bar per line at 20 °C (RCT catalogue page, THOMAFLUID multi-lumen coupling).
- Cam-and-groove couplings are made to interchangeable standards (A-A-59326, which replaced MIL-C-27487; BS EN 14420-7; DIN 2828) in sizes from 1/2 in to 8 in, so halves from different makers fit (Wikipedia 'Cam and groove').

## Common failures

Leaks because the tube wasn't pushed in far enough to pass the O-ring (the most common cause), a slanted cut, a sharp tube end that nicked the O-ring, or a side load that squashed the O-ring oval (Fresh Water Systems); fix by re-cutting square and pushing fully home with a depth mark. Tube works loose or slips out: tube too soft, undersized or scored (Clippard; John Guest via US Plastic), frequent removal scratching the tube (Fresh Water Systems), pressure surges (SMC), or on 3D printers the constant push-pull of retraction and vibration pushing the release collar in; a collet clip under the collar stops this (Printables 135121). A tube re-used without cutting off the marked end, or swollen polyurethane tube, won't reseal or re-insert (SMC KQG2 manual). Fittings crack if the tube is rotated, levered or vibrated at the fitting (SMC). Quick couplers: mismatched plug profiles latch but leak (Wikipedia, Air-line fitting).

## Safety

Always release the pressure before disconnecting (John Guest via US Plastic; SMC KQG2 manual): a hose or tube freed under pressure can whip about, and ISO 4414 'safety' couplings vent the air in two stages before releasing for this reason (Wikipedia, Air-line fitting). With single shut-off couplings, only one half seals, so the other half may still spray or vent (Midstate). Pressure spikes (surge) above the rating can push the tube out (SMC). Near welding spatter, ordinary plastic fittings and tubing are a fire risk; flame-resistant ones exist (SMC KQG2 manual). Printed fittings should not be used for pressurised gas where a failure could hurt someone ⚑ [uncertain: general caution, no test found]. Cam-and-groove (camlock) couplings 'are not recommended for any type of compressed gas service, including steam or air' (Wikipedia 'Cam and groove'); keep them for liquids, and fit the lever safety pins where the coupling has them.

## Related

- Hose barb: a ridged spigot inside soft tubing; cheaper and seals better on soft tube, but hard to undo (Clippard)
- Compression fitting: a nut squeezes a ferrule onto hard plastic or metal tube; needs spanners but takes higher pressure and vibration ⚑ [uncertain: not verified in this entry's sources]
- Bayonet mount (push-and-twist lock): the idea behind quarter-turn hose couplings such as Storz and GEKA (Wikipedia, Hose coupling)
- Cam-and-groove (camlock) coupling: covered in this entry as a lever-locked variant for large hoses; two cam arms clamp a grooved adapter against a gasket and lock over-centre (Wikipedia, Hose coupling; Wikipedia 'Cam and groove')
- Band flange clamp (tri-clamp): a hinged clamp squeezes two flanged ends onto a gasket, for sanitary pipes (Wikipedia, Hose coupling)
- Quick-release pin (ball-lock pin): uses the same spring-loaded balls to lock a pin instead of a hose plug
- Slip ring and rotary union: when the tube must keep turning, a rotary joint is needed instead (SMC recommends rotary fittings)
- Cable [gland](../glossary.md#gland) and strain relief: seals a cable, not a tube, through a wall
