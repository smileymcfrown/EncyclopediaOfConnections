A two-part fastener in which a stud with a small knob is pressed into a socket that has a springy ring or wire inside, which grips a groove on the stud and holds the two halves together until they are pulled apart by hand.

**In plain words:** A little round metal or plastic button in two halves that you press together until it clicks, and pull apart to open, like the poppers on a baby's bodysuit or a denim jacket. **Proper term:** Snap fastener (press stud).

**Also called:** press stud, popper, snap, snap button, press button, press fastener, dome fastener (Wikipedia 'Snap fastener'); tich, tich button (British/regional; Wikipedia); Druckknopf (German); ring snap, utility snap, line snap, ring-spring snap (heavier snaps whose socket holds a split ring spring; KAMsnaps; YKK); spring snap, S-spring snap, parallel-spring snap (socket holds two wires kept in place by a small spring; Kat Makes; Rome Fastener); open-ring snap, prong snap, pronged snap, gripper snap (the cap has pointed prongs that push through the fabric; KAMsnaps; Kat Makes); sew-on snap (flat snaps stitched on by hand; Kat Makes); plastic resin snap, KAM snap (KAM is a brand; KAMsnaps); screw stud, cloth-to-surface snap (a stud that screws into a boat deck or wall so fabric can snap onto it; canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com); DOT Durable, Baby Durable, Line 20 / Line 24 (brand and product-line names from Scovill / DOT, often used as generic sizes); Lift-the-DOT and Pull-the-DOT (brands): directional locking snaps that only open when lifted or pulled at the marked side (Wikipedia).

## How it works

One half carries a stud with a rounded head and a narrow neck (groove) under it; the other half is a socket with a springy ring or pair of wires just inside its opening (Rome Fastener: 'the spring in the socket grips a groove in the stud to hold both sides together'). Pressing the halves together pushes the spring aside as the head passes, then the spring closes back into the groove, giving the click; a firm pull spreads the spring again and the halves separate (Wikipedia: a lip fits into a groove, 'holding them fast until a certain amount of force is applied'). Ring-spring sockets give a stiff, strong hold for heavy fabric and leather, while spring (S-wire) sockets 'gently hang on to the stud' for a softer action (Kat Makes; KAMsnaps). Directional versions such as Lift-the-DOT lock on three sides and only release when the marked side is lifted (Wikipedia).

## Parts

A few parts: one closure usually has four pieces, a socket and a stud (the working halves) each fixed to the material by an eyelet (post) or a decorative cap (Rome Fastener; Wikipedia gallery 'Rivet snap parts'); the socket contains the spring (a round split-ring spring, a parallel 'S' spring of two wires, or a segmented spring; Rome Fastener). Sew-on snaps are just two flat halves with holes for thread; pronged snaps use a pronged cap or ring instead of an eyelet.

## Everyday examples

- Poppers on baby bodysuits and children's clothes (Wikipedia; CPSC recall notices)
- Pearl snaps on Western shirts and snaps on denim jackets and anoraks (Wikipedia; Kat Makes)
- Boat covers, bimini tops and tonneau covers snapped onto screw studs (canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com)
- The 10 mm stud on an antistatic wrist strap, and snap closures on wallets, bags and holsters (Wikipedia gallery; Printables wallet repair)

## When to use it

Use press studs when fabric, leather, canvas or thin flexible sheet has to close and open quickly, by hand, many times: clothing, baby clothes, bags, wallets, holsters, covers, straps and pouches. They are fast to open, don't snag (Wikipedia), look neat, and a kit makes them easy for a beginner to fit. Use screw studs to snap fabric onto a boat, wall or other hard surface, and directional Lift-the-DOT or Pull-the-DOT snaps where covers must not pop open in the wind (Wikipedia).

## When not to use it

Avoid them for joining rigid parts that carry real loads (use screws, a snap fit designed into the parts, or a latch), for anything that must seal (use a zip with a flap, a roll-top or a gasket), where the fastener will be peeled or tugged often in normal use (it will pop open; use a buckle, hook or zip), and on very thin or delicate fabric with stiff ring snaps (the fabric can tear first; use spring snaps, plastic snaps or sew-on snaps; Kat Makes). For adjustable closures use hook-and-loop fastener or a ladder-lock buckle; for one-handed, blind closing a magnetic snap may be easier.

## Details

- **What it does (jobs):**
  - join and hold: holds two layers of fabric, leather, canvas or thin sheet together
  - lock, and release by hand: opened with a pull, no tools; directional types (Lift-the-DOT, Pull-the-DOT) only open when lifted or pulled at the marked side (Wikipedia; Components Direct)
- **Movement:** none: it is a closed fastener, though a round stud in a round socket usually lets the two halves swivel around the stud's axis
- **Directions it can move (degrees of freedom):** 0 for most uses (it just holds the two layers together); in practice the snapped halves can usually spin around the stud (1 rotation), because both the stud and the socket are round
- **Special behaviour:**
  - quick-release: pulls apart by hand in a moment, which is why it was used on military and paratroop equipment, holsters and children's clothing (Wikipedia)
  - [fail-safe](../glossary.md#fail-safe--fail-secure) or breakaway: a snagged snap lets go before the garment or wearer is dragged; Wikipedia says rodeo cowboys adopted them so a shirt snagged on the saddle would come undone, but that claim is marked 'citation needed' ⚑ [uncertain]
  - hand-release only: directional Lift-the-DOT and Pull-the-DOT snaps lock on three sides and only open when lifted or pulled at the marked side (Wikipedia; canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com)
- **How it holds:**
  - shape (the parts interlock; [form-fit](../glossary.md#form-fit--force-fit--material-bond)): the head of the stud sits behind the ring or wires in the socket
  - spring force (a spring or springy part presses it in place): the split ring or S-wire spring in the socket presses into the stud's groove (Rome Fastener)
- **Input and output:** not applicable
- **Power:** passive (no power needed)
- **Load:** light: fine for clothing, bags, covers and straps, strongest against sideways sliding (shear) of the two layers and weakest against a straight [peel](../glossary.md#peel-vs-shear) or pull on the fabric next to it, which is exactly how it is opened ⚑ [uncertain]. Heavier ring-spring and 'Durable' snaps hold much more than spring or pronged snaps (KAMsnaps; Scovill). No reliable holding-force figures were found; the pull needed to unsnap, both straight out and sideways, is measured with ASTM D4846 'Standard Test Method for Resistance to Unsnapping of Snap Fasteners' (ASTM store page), but results depend on brand, size and fabric.
- **Play and precision:** noticeable: the halves can swivel and wobble slightly, and a loose crimp lets the socket or stud spin on the material, which strains it (canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com); it tolerates only small [misalignment](../glossary.md#misalignment) because the stud must enter the socket squarely ⚑ [uncertain]
- **Taking it apart:** tool-free quick release: the two halves snap apart by hand as often as you like; the halves themselves are set permanently into the material by riveting (rolling or crushing the post), prongs or stitching, so removing a set snap usually destroys it (Rome Fastener; Wikipedia)
- **How often it can be reused:** effectively unlimited opening and closing for the life of the garment or cover (DOT says Durable fasteners give 'a firm grip that will remain secure through years of use'; Scovill); fitting to the material is once only
- **Sealing:** none: it only holds layers together; water and dust can pass around it
- **Adjustable:** no: position is fixed once set; you can fit several studs in a row for different closing positions ⚑ [uncertain]; a printed design with several notches on one stud holds materials of different thickness (Printables 'Variable Snap Button Fastener')
- **Access needed:** both sides to fit a rivet-style or pronged snap, because the setter and anvil must be on opposite sides of the material; one side only for a screw stud screwed into a hard surface (canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com); one side only to open or close
- **Materials:** Usually brass (often nickel-plated), steel, stainless steel (316 stainless for marine and corrosive use; Components Direct), brass/stainless and brass/plastic combinations, or all plastic (Scovill DOT Durable page; KAMsnaps). Suits fabric, leather, canvas, vinyl and webbing; open-ring (pronged) snaps suit thin and stretchy fabrics but not 'tough, thick or dense material' (KAMsnaps). Screw studs let fabric snap onto fibreglass, wood or metal. 3D-printed snaps work in PLA or PETG (Printables models).
- **Heat, cold, water and wear:** Salt water corrodes plated steel and can dull brass: stainless steel 'holds its finish better and is stronger than the brass crimp' (canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com), and 316 stainless snaps are made for harsh, corrosive environments (Components Direct). Directional Pull-the-DOT and Lift-the-DOT snaps need lubricating as they age, the latter about twice a year (canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com). Plastic snaps and printed snaps can soften in heat and become [brittle](../glossary.md#brittle--ductile) in cold, and the springs lose grip over years of use ⚑ [uncertain].

## Making it

- **Ways to make or get it:**
  - off-the-shelf part: the normal choice; metal or plastic snaps set with a hand tool, pliers or press
  - sewing and leatherwork: sew-on snaps, or rivet-style snaps set through fabric or leather
  - 3D print (FDM): printed sew-on or snap-together poppers in PLA or PETG (Printables models)
  - woodwork / boat work: screw studs screwed into hard surfaces
- **Printing it in one piece (print in place):** no: the stud and socket halves are printed as separate parts, then sewn, glued or screwed on and snapped together
- **Tools:** To fit: a setting tool (a punch-like 'setter' and a shaped anvil) plus a hammer or mallet for rivet-style snaps (Wikipedia; Kat Makes), or snap pliers or a bench/hand press (Wikipedia; Scovill); plastic KAM snaps need special pliers or a snap press (Kat Makes); sew-on snaps need only a needle and thread; a hole punch or cutter for heavy canvas unless the snap is self-piercing (DOT Durable is self-piercing; Components Direct). To open: none.
- **Difficulty:** easy: buy a kit with the setting tool and hammer them on, after a test on scrap; printed snaps are also easy but weaker

### 3D printing tips

1) A printed popper is really a small round snap-together bead and groove (an annular snap fit), so the annular snap fit entry's design rules apply ⚑ [uncertain]. 2) Print both halves flat on the bed with no supports; makers report good results at 0.15 mm layers, in PETG with a 0.6 mm nozzle (volly's 'very flat snap fastener' for a wallet, used for two months) or in PLA with 5% [infill](../glossary.md#infill) (nortmann_c's 'Snap Button'), and in PLA without brim for a strap extender (Vik's parametric 10 mm popper extension). 3) Expect the first press to need 'quite some force' (nortmann_c), then the fit eases. 4) Use PETG or another tough material for snaps opened often; PLA is stiffer and more brittle, so its snap ring can crack ⚑ [uncertain]. 5) To join fabric to a print, you can print a sew-on popper with thread holes, print a matching half that snaps onto a real metal snap (SmilingImpact's 'marine snap fastener' is made to connect to real marine snaps), or screw a metal screw stud into the print ⚑ [uncertain]. 6) Print a test pair first and adjust the bead or groove size by 0.1-0.2 mm until the click feels right ⚑ [uncertain].

## Rules of thumb

- Sizes are given in lignes (lines): 1 ligne = 0.635 mm, so line 14 is about 9 mm, line 16 about 10 mm, line 20 about 12-12.7 mm and line 24 about 15 mm (Ata Buttons size chart; KAMsnaps).
- Use line 20 for general use and line 24 for thicker material and heavy-duty use; open-ring (pronged) snaps are for thin material and are 'not suited for tough, thick or dense material' (KAMsnaps).
- All four parts (cap, socket, stud, post) must come from the same size series: 'Mixing sizes will cause snap failure' (Ata Buttons size chart).
- Choose the eyelet or post length to suit the total material thickness; makers sell 'a range of post lengths to accommodate a variety of material thicknesses' (Rome Fastener; Scovill DOT Durable page).
- Stiff ring (utility) snaps on delicate fabric can tear it: 'if you pull on the fabric to un-snap the snap, the fabric will rip before the snap comes apart' - use spring or plastic snaps, or add interfacing, on thin fabrics (Kat Makes).
- Add a layer of interfacing behind pronged snaps on delicate fabrics (Kat Makes).
- A properly set snap should not spin: 'a snug crimp puts less strain on the snap and the material' (canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com).

## Common failures

The snap pulls out of the fabric or tears it (thin fabric, no interfacing, too stiff a ring snap, or a post too short for the thickness; Kat Makes; Rome Fastener). The snap spins or wobbles because the crimp is loose (canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com). It won't close, or pops open, because parts from different size series were mixed (Ata Buttons) or the socket was crushed while setting (spring snaps need a special tool 'to avoid crushing'; Kat Makes). The spring weakens and the snap opens too easily after long use ⚑ [uncertain]. Marine snaps seize or corrode (lubricate and use stainless; canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com). People tug on a directional Pull-the-DOT snap from the wrong side and tear the canvas, and the tiny spring in a Lift-the-DOT socket can get sprung so it no longer fits over the stud (canvas-boat-cover-and-repair-advisor.com). On baby clothes, snaps detach (CPSC).

## Safety

Snaps that come off baby clothes are a choking hazard, and broken ones can expose small sharp prongs: the US CPSC has recalled infant bodysuits because 'the snaps ... can detach and also expose small sharp prongs, posing choking and laceration hazards to young children' (CPSC recall, 5 May 2022). Set snaps firmly, test them, and check baby clothes for loose snaps. Keep fingers clear of the setter when hammering.

## Related

- Hook-and-loop fastener: grips over an area and is adjustable, but is noisy, collects lint and grips less positively
- Basic strap and garment closures (prong buckle, button, toggle and loop): cheaper and sewn, but slower to open and close
- Magnet joint (embedded magnets, magnetic catch) and magnetic snaps: close blind and one-handed, but hold less against a direct pull
- Annular snap fit: the same bead-and-groove idea moulded or printed into rigid round plastic parts
- [Cantilever](../glossary.md#cantilever) snap fit: a springy hook for joining rigid plastic parts
- Side-release buckle: stronger and quick-release for straps and webbing
- Spring-gate hook (carabiner, snap hook, lobster clasp): for loops and rings rather than flat layers
- Ball [detent](../glossary.md#detent) (spring plunger): a spring-loaded ball in a groove, the same 'spring into a groove' principle used to click parts into positions
- Spring-steel push-on fasteners (push nut, speed nut, U-nut): the ball-stud speed clip is a sheet-metal cousin that snaps panels onto a ball stud
