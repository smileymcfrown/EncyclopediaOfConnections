A shim, spacer or standoff is a piece of known thickness or length put between two parts, so that the gap, height or spacing between them is set by that piece and then held when the parts are bolted or pressed together.

**In plain words:** Thin packing pieces, washers or little tubes that you put between two parts to fill a gap, raise one part to the right height, or hold a circuit board up off a surface. **Proper term:** Shims, spacers and standoffs (packing, spacer washer).

**Also called:** shim (Wikipedia 'Shim (spacer)'); packing, packer or packing piece (UK workshop usage) ⚑ [uncertain: no opened source]; shim stock / shimstock (thin sheet or foil cut to size); pre-cut shim / slotted shim / U-shaped shim (machine alignment shims that slide round a bolt); laminated shim / [peel](../glossary.md#peel-vs-shear) shim / peelable shim / peel-off shim (stacked foil layers peeled off one at a time); separable shim (layers held by glue spots on the edge only); spacer / spacer tube / spacer sleeve / unthreaded spacer / round spacer; spacer washer / plain washer used as a spacer / height washer (rowing oarlocks); standoff / stand-off / PCB standoff / hex standoff / threaded spacer / pillar / PCB support; male-female standoff, female-female standoff (Wikipedia 'Spacers and standoffs'); motherboard standoff / brass standoff (PCs); insulating standoff / nylon standoff; 'hex nut screw' (Dell's name for standoffs that take thumbscrews) (Wikipedia 'Spacers and standoffs'); also sold as 'jack nut' or 'jack post' for connector jackscrews ⚑ [uncertain: no opened source for these two names]; machinist's jack (a threaded spacer with a bolt in it, used as an adjustable support) (Wikipedia 'Jackscrew'); CPU shim (protects a processor when fitting a heat sink); tile spacer (masonry and tiling).

## How it works

Two parts that are clamped together end up exactly as far apart as whatever is squeezed between them, so putting in a piece of known thickness sets the gap, height or spacing. Flat shims are stacked or swapped until a part sits level, lines up or has the right [clearance](../glossary.md#clearance); a peel shim is a stack of bonded foils, and you peel off layers until it is the right thickness. Spacers and spacer washers are tubes or rings that a bolt passes through, so they hold two parts a set distance apart when the bolt is tightened. A standoff is a threaded pillar: one end screws to the base and the other takes the screw that holds a circuit board or panel, lifting it clear so solder joints on the back can't touch the surface (and, if it is nylon, insulating it).

## Parts

Single piece in most cases: a flat shim, a washer or a spacer tube. A laminated (peel) shim is one piece made of many bonded foil layers. A standoff is one threaded pillar, used with one or two screws (e.g. 'standoff, two M3 screws, circuit board, base plate'). A machinist's jack is a few parts: a threaded spacer and a bolt.

## Everyday examples

- a folded piece of paper or card pushed under a wobbly table leg (Wikipedia 'Shim (spacer)')
- the brass standoffs that hold a computer motherboard off the metal case, or the M2.5 standoffs that stack an add-on board on a Raspberry Pi
- thin shims behind a door hinge to stop the door rubbing (Printables 146015 'Door Hinge Shim Set')
- a shim under a bolt-on guitar neck, or under the nut or saddle, to change string height (Wikipedia 'Shim (spacer)')

## When to use it

Whenever something is slightly too low, too loose, not level or out of line and the parts are (or can be) clamped together: under machine and motor feet, behind hinges and brackets, under furniture legs, in a bearing housing to set end float, or between a gear and a housing. Use spacers or spacer washers to hold two parts a fixed distance apart on a bolt, e.g. a pulley clear of a frame. Use standoffs to mount circuit boards, displays and panels clear of a case or plate, to stack boards, and (in nylon) to insulate them. It is the cheapest, simplest adjustment there is and needs no design changes.

## When not to use it

When the adjustment must be made often or continuously: use a levelling foot, locking adjustment screws (push-pull screws) or a slotted mount with a jacking screw instead. For a tapered gap or a quick tight fit under load, use a wedge ([Self-locking](../glossary.md#self-locking) wedge, which covers tapered shims and folding wedges). For a gap that must also seal, use a gasket. Where the parts must be located exactly and repeatably without fiddling, design in locating features (locating pins, [kinematic coupling](../glossary.md#kinematic-coupling), exact-constraint mounting). Don't use many thin shims, or soft paper, wood or PLA shims, under heavy or vibrating machinery: they compress and cause soft foot; use a few metal shims or one machined block.

## Details

- **What it does (jobs):**
  - line parts up exactly: sets a height, gap, level or alignment, e.g. under a motor foot, a wobbly table leg or a guitar neck
  - join and hold: standoffs and spacers are part of the screwed joint that holds a circuit board or panel a set distance from its base
- **Movement:** none: once clamped the parts don't move; a shim can be changed later to re-adjust
- **Directions it can move (degrees of freedom):** 0 once clamped. The shim or spacer fixes the distance along the bolt; the bolt and clamping friction stop the parts moving the other ways.
- **Special behaviour:**
  - none: it simply fills a gap with a known thickness. The 'adjustment' happens by choosing, stacking or peeling shims, not by any mechanism
- **How it holds:** friction or clamping (force-fit): shims and spacers only work when squeezed by bolts or by the weight of the part; plus shape ([form-fit](../glossary.md#form-fit--force-fit--material-bond)) for standoffs, which screw into threads
- **Input and output:** not applicable
- **Power:** passive (no power needed)
- **Load:** Light to heavy, depending on material: metal shims under machine feet carry the full weight of motors and pumps in compression. Strong when squeezed (compression); they carry no pull (tension) themselves and resist sideways shear only by clamping friction and the bolt. Standoffs are light-duty: they hold circuit boards and panels and are weak in bending and at their small threads. Paper, card, wood and printed shims are light-duty and crush or [creep](../glossary.md#creep) under a constant heavy load.
- **Play and precision:** No play once clamped. Precision depends on the shims: machine-alignment shims are made to small [tolerances](../glossary.md#tolerance) (ground stock about +/-0.0005 in for 0.020 in and thinner; rolled stock about +/-0.005 in for 0.025 in and thicker, according to Acoem), but every extra layer, plus dirt, rust or paint, adds error and springiness, so use as few as possible. Peel shims adjust in 0.05 to 0.1 mm steps (Jicey; ERIKS). Spacers and standoffs locate only along the bolt; sideways position comes from the holes.
- **Taking it apart:** removable: undo the bolts and slide out, add or swap shims; standoffs unscrew
- **How often it can be reused:** many times for metal shims, washers and standoffs; a few times for paper, card, wood and printed shims, which crush, creep or crack; peel shims can only get thinner (layers once peeled can't be put back)
- **Sealing:** none: shims and spacers leave or set a gap; to seal a joint use a gasket or sealing washer instead
- **Adjustable:** yes, by changing the shims: add, remove, swap or peel layers off a laminated shim, then re-tighten; spacer height is changed by swapping to a different length or stacking spacers or washers
- **Access needed:** one side only (blind) for slip-in shims (they slide in from the side) and for a male-female standoff screwed into a tapped hole; both sides for a female-female standoff or a spacer with a through bolt and nut
- **Materials:** Shims: almost anything flat: steel, stainless steel, brass, aluminium, plastic film, wood, stone, card or paper (Wikipedia 'Shim (spacer)'). Pre-cut stainless steel shims are standard for machine alignment because they don't rust (Acoem). Laminated peel shims: steel, aluminium, brass, stainless steel and titanium foils, or polyester (Mylar) and polyimide (Kapton) films for electrical insulation (Jicey). Spacers and standoffs: stainless steel, aluminium, brass or nylon (Wikipedia 'Spacers and standoffs'); washers also in copper, zinc-plated steel, PTFE, phenolic, fibre and mica (Wikipedia 'Washer (hardware)'). Printed spacers: PETG or ASA rather than PLA where warm. They suit any material being joined, as long as the clamping force doesn't crush a soft shim.
- **Heat, cold, water and wear:** Carbon-steel shims rust, and rust under a foot changes the height ('dirt is a shim'), so stainless shims are preferred (Acoem; LUDECA). Plastic-film peel shims work from about -70 °C to +220 °C (polyester) and above 400 °C (polyimide) (Jicey). Printed spacers soften from about 60 °C (PLA), 80 °C (PETG) or 110 °C (ASA) under load (CNC Kitchen) and creep under a constant clamp load, so bolts through them can loosen over time (Thrinter). Paper, card and wood shims compress and swell or shrink with damp ⚑ [uncertain: no opened source]. Vibration can walk loose shims out if they are not clamped ⚑ [uncertain].

## Making it

- **Ways to make or get it:**
  - off-the-shelf part (washers, spacer tubes, hex standoffs, pre-cut and laminated shims, shim stock)
  - 3D print (FDM): spacers, step shims, printed standoffs (with [heat-set inserts](../glossary.md#heat-set-insert) or self-tapping screws)
  - 3D print (resin): small threaded or self-tapping standoffs (Printables 601883 was resin-printed)
  - sheet metal: cut shims from shim stock or thin sheet with scissors or snips
  - laser cut: acrylic or plywood spacers and shim plates
  - woodwork: wooden packers and wedges
- **Printing it in one piece (print in place):** yes for a plain printed spacer, washer or shim: it is one solid piece that works straight off the bed; partly for a printed standoff, which needs screws or a heat-set insert to hold the board
- **Tools:** none to slide in a shim under a part; spanner or screwdriver for the bolts that clamp it; nut driver or small spanner for hex standoffs; scissors or tin snips to cut shim stock; a micrometer or caliper (or feeler gauge) to measure the gap and the shim stack
- **Difficulty:** easy: buy washers, spacers and standoffs, cut shims from shim stock, drink cans or card, or print spacers in one piece

### 3D printing tips

Print spacers and standoffs standing up, so the hole is a true circle in the XY plane and the layers are squeezed (not split) by the clamping bolt ⚑ [uncertain: general practice, no opened page]. Use plenty of walls and high [infill](../glossary.md#infill) so the spacer doesn't crush: makers use about 80% infill for step shims (Printables 355004) and 100% for workshop table shims (Printables 777364). Make the hole slightly bigger than the screw (e.g. 3.2 mm for M3, as in the 'Spacer Kit' Printables 1182373) or, for a self-tapping standoff, slightly smaller (2.9 mm for M3 in Printables 601883). For threaded standoffs that are undone often, melt in a heat-set insert instead of tapping plastic. Choose the material for the heat: in an oven test under a small load, PLA started to give at about 60 °C, PETG at about 80 °C and ASA at about 110 °C (CNC Kitchen); and plastic under a constant clamp load slowly creeps (Thrinter), so a bolt through a printed spacer can lose tightness over time. For accurate gap-setting, print a shim sizer or gauge first (Printables 175528) and remember a printed shim's thickness is only as accurate as your first layer and layer height. Don't use printed parts as shims under heavy machinery: use metal.

## Rules of thumb

- Use as few shims as possible: 'Three pre-cut shims should always be enough; in extraordinary situations, four could be needed', because too many shims can themselves cause a rocking 'soft foot' (Easy-Laser via LUDECA, https://ludeca.com/blog/alignment/10213/5-dos-and-donts-of-shimming/). Acoem says to try to use no more than 4 under each foot, and to use one thick ground steel block plus a few shims for big gaps (https://acoem.us/blog/shaft-alignment/shims-101-for-shaft-alignment/).
- 'Dirt is a shim – as is rust, paint, sand': clean under the foot, measure the stack with a micrometer or caliper instead of trusting the number stamped on the shim, and sandwich thin shims between thicker ones (Acoem, https://acoem.us/blog/shaft-alignment/shims-101-for-shaft-alignment/).
- Slide slotted shims in until they touch the bolt, then pull them back slightly so the bolt threads don't bend them when tightened (Acoem, https://acoem.us/blog/shaft-alignment/shims-101-for-shaft-alignment/).
- Peel shims: a standard 1 mm U-shaped laminated shim is made of 8 layers of 0.1 mm and 4 of 0.05 mm, peeled until the exact thickness is reached (Jicey, https://jicey.com/shims.html; ERIKS BETEX, https://shop.eriks.be/en/peel-off-shims-hard-brass-or-stainless-steel-pr451749624000/1/).
- Match the standoff thread to the board: Raspberry Pi mounting holes take M2.5, and 11 mm standoffs give the right spacing for an add-on board (HAT) on standard female headers (Pololu, https://www.pololu.com/product/1952); PC motherboards use 6-32 standoffs and D-sub connector jackscrews use 4-40 (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacers_and_standoffs).
- Fit a standoff only under a matching board hole; remove any extra ones, because 'a standoff installed in the wrong place may cause electrical shorts' (ASRock install guide, https://download.asrock.com/Manual/InstallEATXMB/Install%20EATX%20Motherboard.pdf).
- Use a washer to spread a bolt's load on soft material; hardened steel washers keep the bolt tight in high-quality joints, and insulating washers prevent galvanic corrosion between steel screws and aluminium (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washer_(hardware)).
- Printed spacers soften under load at about 60 °C (PLA), 80 °C (PETG) and 110 °C (ASA) (CNC Kitchen, https://www.cnckitchen.com/blog/comparing-pla-petg-amp-asa-feat-prusament).

## Common failures

(1) Too many thin shims make a springy stack that lets the part rock or move when bolts are tightened (soft foot): use three or four at most (LUDECA; Acoem). (2) Dirt, paint or rust under the shim changes the height: clean first (Acoem). (3) Trusting the stamped thickness: measure the stack (Acoem). (4) A shim bent by the bolt threads: pull it back slightly from the bolt (Acoem). (5) Soft shims (paper, card, wood, PLA) crush or creep and the part sinks or the bolt loosens: use metal or a stiffer plastic. (6) A shim that only touches part of the foot leaves it rocking (angular soft foot); a single shim cut to fit the wedge-shaped gap is better than a stair-step of shims (Acoem). (7) An extra or misplaced metal standoff shorts a circuit board (ASRock). (8) Wrong standoff thread or length (e.g. M3 instead of M2.5, or too short for the connector headers between stacked boards) (Pololu).

## Safety

Thin metal shims and cut shim stock have sharp edges and corners: handle with gloves and deburr cut edges (Acoem; LUDECA recommends burr-free, rounded-corner shims). Lock out (switch off and isolate) a machine before putting your hands under its feet (LUDECA). Metal standoffs fitted where there is no board hole can short-circuit a circuit board (ASRock), and metal standoffs and washers connect the board's mounting holes to the case, so use nylon ones where the board must stay insulated (Wikipedia 'Spacers and standoffs').

## Related

- Self-locking wedge (wedge clamp, [taper](../glossary.md#taper) key): tapered shims and folding wedges are covered there; they set a gap by sliding a taper rather than by stacking flat pieces
- Eccentric adjuster (eccentric nut, spacer or bushing): an off-centre spacer turned to shift a part sideways, then clamped
- Levelling foot (adjustable foot, swivel levelling pad): screw-adjusted height instead of packing under a leg
- Locking adjustment screws (push-pull screws, spring-loaded levelling screws): fine, repeatable height or tilt adjustment without loose shims
- Slotted adjustment with a jacking screw (slotted mount, sliding motor plate): adjusts position sideways along slots
- Tolerance ring (wavy-band press fit): an 'elastic shim' that takes up a [radial](../glossary.md#axial--radial) gap between a shaft and a hole
- Wave and disc springs (compact axial springs): spring washers that take up end float with a spring force instead of a fixed thickness
- Thread-locking hardware (nylon-insert nut, lock washer, split pin): lock washers stop loosening; plain washers only spread load or space parts
- Gasket (flat, cord-in-groove or cast-in-place): looks like a washer or shim but is made to seal
- Heat-set threaded insert: gives a 3D-printed part metal threads for standoff screws
- Exact-constraint mounting (3-2-1 locating) and Locating pins and hole-and-slot: locate parts exactly by design rather than by shimming
